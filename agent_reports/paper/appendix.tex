\subsection{Algorithm pseudocode}
\label{app:algorithms}

We provide complete pseudocode for the two algorithmic contributions of
the paper: Semantic PER with the failure-direction signal
(Algorithm~\ref{alg:sem-per}) and VLM-Verified Counterfactual Hindsight
(Algorithm~\ref{alg:verified-cf}). Both are presented in
numbered-line form with the SAC training loop made explicit; the
companion symbol glossary (Table~\ref{tab:notation}) lists every variable
used. We have intentionally written the two algorithms so that
Algorithm~\ref{alg:verified-cf} is a drop-in replacement for the
\emph{HER relabel step} of any SAC+HER trainer --- a reimplementer only
needs to (a) maintain a forkable second env instance and (b) gate the
hindsight-goal channel behind the simulator-rollout test of
lines~5--10 below.

$\theta, \bar\theta$; sum-tree priorities $\{p_i\}$;

\quad\textsc{1:}\;\textbf{for} env-step $n\!=\!1,\dots,n_{\max}$ \textbf{do}\\
\quad\textsc{2:}\quad\;$a_t\!\sim\!\pi_\theta(\cdot\!\mid\!s_t)$;
$(r_t,s_{t+1})\!\leftarrow\!E.\texttt{step}(a_t)$\\
\quad\textsc{3:}\quad\;Push $(s_t,a_t,r_t,s_{t+1})$ at slot $i_t$ with
$p_{i_t}\!\leftarrow\!\max_j p_j$, $w_{i_t}\!\leftarrow\!1$ \hfill\textit{(PER max-prio init)}\\
\quad\textsc{4:}\quad\;\textbf{if} episode ends at step $t$ \textbf{then}\\
\quad\textsc{5:}\quad\quad\;\textbf{if} $r_t\!<\!0$ \textit{(failed episode)} \textbf{then}\\

\quad\textsc{9:}\quad\quad\quad\quad\;$w_i\!\leftarrow\!w_{\max}$ \textbf{if} $|\tau_i\!-\!t_{\text{fail}}|\!\le\!W$ \textbf{else} $1$\\

\quad\quad\;\textbf{end if} \hfill\textit{(closes line~5 failed-episode branch)}\\
\quad\;\textbf{end if} \hfill\textit{(closes line~4 episode-end check)}\\
\quad\textsc{11:}\quad\;\textbf{if} $n>n_{\text{warm-up}}$ \textbf{and} $n \bmod n_{\text{update}}\!=\!0$ \textbf{then}\\
\quad\textsc{12:}\quad\quad\;Sample $\{i_j\}_{j=1}^{B}\!\sim\!\mu(i)\!\propto\!p_i$ via sum-tree\\
\quad\textsc{13:}\quad\quad\;$w_{\text{IS},j}\!\leftarrow\!(|\mathcal{D}_B|\!\cdot\!\mu(i_j))^{-\beta_t}\big/\max_{j'}(\cdot)$\\
\quad\textsc{14:}\quad\quad\;$\delta_{i_j}\!\leftarrow\!r_{i_j}\!+\!\gamma\,Q_{\bar\theta}(s_{i_j+1},\pi_\theta(s_{i_j+1}))\!-\!Q_\theta(s_{i_j},a_{i_j})$\\
\quad\textsc{15:}\quad\quad\;$\mathcal{L}_Q\!\leftarrow\!\tfrac{1}{B}\!\sum_j w_{\text{IS},j}\,\delta_{i_j}^{2}$;
update $\theta$ via Adam\\
\quad\textsc{16:}\quad\quad\;Update $\pi_\theta$ and $\bar\theta$ per standard SAC \hfill\textit{(actor + Polyak)}\\
\quad\textsc{17:}\quad\quad\;\textbf{for} $j\!=\!1,\dots,B$ \textbf{do}
$\;p_{i_j}\!\leftarrow\!(|\delta_{i_j}|\!+\!\varepsilon)^\alpha\!\cdot\!w_{i_j}$;
write to sum-tree leaf $i_j$ \hfill\textit{(TD-driven refresh of sampled slots)}\\
\quad\quad\quad\;\textbf{end for} \hfill\textit{(closes line~17 priority refresh)}\\
\quad\quad\;\textbf{end if} \hfill\textit{(closes line~11 update check)}\\
\quad\textsc{18:}\;\textbf{end for} \hfill\textit{(closes line~1 main training loop)}\\[2pt]

\quad\textsc{2:}\;$\hat k\!\leftarrow\!\mathrm{Loc}_\phi(\{F_k\},\,\text{task\_desc},\,T)$;
$\;K^\star\!\leftarrow\!\lfloor \hat k\cdot(T\!-\!1)/(K\!-\!1)\rfloor$ \hfill\textit{(failure step; same linear interp.\ as Alg.~\ref{alg:sem-per} line~7)}\\

\quad\textsc{4:}\;$\text{out}\!\leftarrow\!\mathrm{CF}_\phi(\{F_k\},\,K^\star,\,g,\,\text{task\_desc},\,\text{variant}\!=\!v)$
\hfill\textit{(returns $p_{\text{cf}}\!\in\!\mathbb{R}^3$ and/or $a_{\text{cf}}\!\in\!\mathbb{R}^4$)}\\
\quad\textsc{5:}\;\textbf{if} $v\!\in\!\{$\textsc{achieved\_goal},\textsc{all},\textsc{joint}$\}$ \textbf{and} $\|p_{\text{cf}}\!-\!g\|\!<\!\rho$ \textbf{then}\\
\quad\quad\;\textbf{return} \textsc{reject} (reason: \texttt{teleport\_collapse}) \hfill\textit{(see Sec.~\ref{sec:exp:prompt})}\\
\quad\textsc{6:}\;\textbf{if} $v\!=\!$\textsc{achieved\_goal} \textbf{then} \hfill\textit{(IK from position target)}\\
\quad\quad\;$a_{\text{cf}}\!\leftarrow\!\mathrm{clip}\bigl((p_{\text{cf}}\!-\!\texttt{ee}_{K^\star})/g_P,\,-1,\,1\bigr)$
concat $[\texttt{grip}\!=\!-1]$\\
\quad\textsc{7:}\;\textbf{else} $a_{\text{cf}}\!\leftarrow\!\mathrm{clip}(\text{out}.a_{\text{cf}},\,-1,\,1)$ \hfill\textit{(use VLM's action directly)}\\
\quad\textsc{8:}\;Write $\sigma$ into $E_{\text{verify}}$ (\texttt{qpos},\,\texttt{qvel},\,\texttt{time},\,\texttt{mocap});
call \texttt{mj\_forward(model,data)}; assert $\|\texttt{qpos}\!-\!\texttt{qpos}^{(\sigma)}\|\!=\!0$\\
\quad\textsc{9:}\;\textbf{for} $j\!=\!0,\dots,N\!-\!1$ \textbf{do}\\
\quad\quad\;$(s'_j, r_j)\!\leftarrow\!E_{\text{verify}}.\texttt{step}(a_{\text{cf}})$ \hfill\textit{(under env's unmodified sparse reward)}\\
\quad\quad\;$r_j\!\leftarrow\!E_{\text{verify}}.\texttt{compute\_reward}(\text{ag}_j, g)$\\
\quad\textsc{10:}\;\textbf{if} $\max_j r_j\!\ge\!0$ \textbf{then} \hfill\textit{(verifier reached the goal)}\\
\quad\quad\;$j^\star\!\leftarrow\!\arg\min\{j\,:\,r_j\!\ge\!0\}$;

\quad\quad\;\textbf{return} \textsc{accept}\\
\quad\textsc{11:}\;\textbf{return} \textsc{reject} (reason: \texttt{goal\_not\_reached}) \hfill\textit{(fall back to standard HER)}

\subsection{Heuristic localizer (``Oracle v3'') --- extended geometric reasoning}
\label{app:oracle-v3}

Section~\ref{app:prompts} (Appendix~A.2) of the main text introduces the
\texttt{GoalDistanceLocalizer} in three precedence-ordered phases. Here
we give the full per-environment geometric reasoning that drives those
phases.

\paragraph{FetchPush --- contact-loss detection.}
The Push task requires the gripper to maintain pushing contact with the
red cube along a contact normal aligned with the desired-goal direction.
We define the binary contact predicate
$\mathrm{ct}_t = \mathbb{1}[\|\text{ee}_t - \text{obj}_t\| < 0.05~\mathrm{m}]$
where $\text{ee}_t$ is the end-effector position and $\text{obj}_t$ the
object center. A contact-loss event at step $t$ is a transition
$(\mathrm{ct}_t,\mathrm{ct}_{t+1})=(1,0)$ such that
(i) the run length of $\neg \mathrm{ct}$ in $[t\!+\!1,\dots]$ is $\ge 2$
(filters single-frame contact bounces),
(ii) the object-goal distance $\|\text{obj}_t - g\| > 0.10$\,m
(filters near-success contact lifts),
(iii) the contact-loss timestep is at least 10 steps before the closest-approach
timestep (filters end-of-episode argmin coincidence).
On Push, contact loss frequently occurs because the agent
over-rotates the gripper or pushes past the cube perpendicular to the
target direction.

\paragraph{FetchPickAndPlace --- ballistic vs.\ argmin precedence.}
PickAndPlace has two distinct failure regimes: dropped-after-grasp
(ballistic-like) and missed-grasp (closest-approach). The localizer first
runs the ballistic-throw detector: compute per-step displacements
$d_t = \|\text{obj}_{t+1} - \text{obj}_t\|$, find peak $t_p\!=\!\arg\max d_t$
with $d_{t_p} > 0.008$\,m, and check that the average post-peak velocity
in the window $[t_p\!+\!1, t_p\!+\!7]$ exceeds $0.35\cdot d_{t_p}$. If
true, mark $t_{\text{fail}}\!=\!t_p$ (the drop moment). If false (object
remained ``carried'' or never moved), fall through to argmin: $\arg\min_t
\|\text{obj}_t - g\|$. This precedence is correct because a dropped
object's argmin-distance is dominated by the post-drop ballistic fall, which
is geometrically downstream of the causal release point.

\paragraph{FetchSlide --- release-point detection.}
Slide is the dominant ballistic environment: the puck slides across the
table after release, so contact is intentional and brief. The ballistic
detector almost always fires. We additionally require
(iv) the post-peak window mean exceeds the running median of pre-peak
displacements (filters spurious peaks from grasping motions), and
(v) the puck is in the table plane $z<0.45$\,m at peak time (filters
gripper-only motion). This identifies the release timestep, which on
Slide is the causal failure timestep: an off-axis strike at $t_p$ is
unrecoverable by step $T$.

\paragraph{Argmin fallback.}
If neither (b) nor (c) fires (the object stays still or moves slowly
relative to noise), we return $\arg\min_t \|\text{obj}_t - g\|$. This is
the closest-approach timestep, which on contact tasks where the agent
maintains pushing throughout is a reasonable diagnostic for ``the moment
divergence began''.

\subsection{Counterfactual prompt templates --- full text}
\label{app:cf-prompts}

We reproduce the four counterfactual prompt variants verbatim. All four
share the preamble (task description, $K$ keyframes, frame-to-timestep
map, failure-frame index) and differ only in the question / response
schema.

\paragraph{Common preamble.}
{\small
\begin{verbatim}
You are analyzing a robotic manipulation trajectory.

Task: {task_description}

{K} keyframes from a FAILED episode are shown in chronological order.
Frame-to-timestep mapping:
{frame_index_map}

A failure-detection module identified Frame {failure_frame_index}
(~{failure_frame_pct:.0f}% through the episode) as the critical moment
where the robot's action caused or revealed the failure.
\end{verbatim}}

\paragraph{Variant 1: \textsc{narrative}.}
{\small
\begin{verbatim}
QUESTION: At Frame {failure_frame_index}, what should the robot have
done differently for the episode to succeed? Be specific about the
corrective behaviour in physical terms (e.g. "lift higher before
approach", "close gripper sooner", "push from the opposite side").

Respond with ONLY a JSON object:
{
  "explanation": "<one concise sentence describing the corrective
                  behaviour>",
  "confidence": <float in [0,1]>
}
\end{verbatim}}

\paragraph{Variant 2: \textsc{action}.}
{\small
\begin{verbatim}
The robot's action space is a 4-D vector: (dx, dy, dz, gripper_command).
  - dx, dy, dz in [-1, 1] are end-effector velocity commands.
    Units: 1.0 ~= 5 cm/step. The 'x' axis points away from the robot,
    'y' to the left, 'z' upward.
  - gripper_command in [-1, 1]: -1 = close, +1 = open.

QUESTION: What 4-D corrective action vector should the robot have
executed at Frame {failure_frame_index} (instead of whatever it actually
did) so the episode would have succeeded? Provide a single delta-action
[dx, dy, dz, grip].

Be conservative: prefer small in-distribution deltas. If the failure is
a released-too-early issue, use grip ~= -1. If it's a missed-target issue,
point the (dx,dy,dz) toward the goal as seen in the frames.

Respond with ONLY a JSON object:
{
  "corrective_action": [<dx>, <dy>, <dz>, <grip>],
  "explanation": "<one concise sentence>",
  "confidence": <float in [0,1]>
}
\end{verbatim}}

\paragraph{Variant 3: \textsc{achieved\_goal}.}
{\small
\begin{verbatim}
In the Fetch suite, `achieved_goal` is the current 3D position of the
manipulated object (or the gripper, for FetchReach). The episode
succeeds when ||achieved_goal - desired_goal|| < 5 cm. Workspace coords
range roughly: x in [1.05, 1.55] m, y in [0.4, 1.1] m, z in [0.4, 0.85] m
(the table surface is at z ~= 0.42 m).

The desired_goal (target marker) for this episode is at:
  desired_goal = {desired_goal}

QUESTION: At Frame {failure_frame_index}, what 3D position SHOULD the
object have reached for the episode trajectory to be on track for
success? In other words, propose a hindsight `achieved_goal` for this
frame — a counterfactual location that, had the object been there at
this timestep, would have set the trajectory up to terminate within 5
cm of the desired_goal.

The proposed position must lie inside the Fetch workspace (above the
table).

Respond with ONLY a JSON object:
{
  "corrective_position": [<x>, <y>, <z>],
  "explanation": "<one concise sentence>",
  "confidence": <float in [0,1]>
}
\end{verbatim}}

\paragraph{Variant 4: \textsc{all} (unified).}
{\small
\begin{verbatim}
Context for the action and goal spaces:
  - Action: 4-D delta vector (dx, dy, dz, gripper), components in [-1,1].
    Each unit ~= 5 cm/step end-effector motion; gripper -1=close / +1=open.
  - achieved_goal / desired_goal: 3-D object (or gripper) positions in m.
    The workspace spans roughly x in [1.05,1.55], y in [0.4,1.1],
                                 z in [0.4,0.85].
  - desired_goal for this episode = {desired_goal}.

Provide a unified counterfactual:
  (a) the 3D position the object should have been at this frame
      (`corrective_position`),
  (b) the 4-D action the robot should have executed at this frame
      (`corrective_action`),
  (c) a one-sentence physical explanation.

Respond with ONLY a JSON object:
{
  "corrective_position": [<x>, <y>, <z>],
  "corrective_action": [<dx>, <dy>, <dz>, <grip>],
  "explanation": "<one concise sentence>",
  "confidence": <float in [0,1]>
}
\end{verbatim}}

\paragraph{Task description substitutions.}
The single \texttt{\{task\_description\}} placeholder takes one of three
strings:
\begin{itemize}\itemsep0pt
\item \texttt{FetchPush}: ``the robot should push the red cube to the green target on the table''.
\item \texttt{FetchPickAndPlace}: ``the robot should pick up the red cube and place it at the floating green target''.
\item \texttt{FetchSlide}: ``the robot should strike the red puck so that it slides to the green target''.
\end{itemize}

\section{Theoretical Derivations}
\label{app:theory}

\subsection{Importance-sampled posterior reweighting --- full derivation}
\label{app:is-derivation}

This section gives the step-by-step IS argument referenced in
Section~\ref{sec:theory}. The derivation itself is standard
importance-sampling applied to a prioritized-replay regression
objective; the framing contribution is the identification of the
VLM posterior $q_\phi$ as a per-trajectory proposal modifier and the
location of the resulting estimator in the off-policy correction
taxonomy (Table~\ref{tab:taxonomy}). We work throughout over a finite
buffer $\mathcal{D}_B$ of size $N\!:=\!|\mathcal{D}_B|$ and write
expectations as discrete sums; $\delta_i(\theta)$ is the per-slot TD error
under critic $\theta$ and $\ell$ is a fixed scalar loss (squared error
or Huber). Self-normalization---rescaling weights by their batch
maximum---is a numerical-stability device applied at minibatch level and
is treated separately at the end of Step~3.

\paragraph{Step 1: the target objective.}
Off-policy value learning targets the Bellman-residual loss under
\emph{uniform replay}:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_U(\theta) \;=\; \mathbb{E}_{i\sim\mu_U}\bigl[\ell(\delta_i(\theta))\bigr]
\;=\; \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N} \ell(\delta_i(\theta)),
\qquad \mu_U(i)=1/N,
\label{eq:target}
\end{equation}
where the empirical mean is over the current contents of $\mathcal{D}_B$.
The gradient of \eqref{eq:target} targets the population gradient under
$\mu_U$ exactly; the construction below modifies the proposal $\mu$ used
at minibatch draw time while keeping $\mathcal{L}_U$ as the estimand.

\paragraph{Step 2: importance-sampled estimator with a non-uniform proposal.}
For any proposal $\mu$ that is \emph{absolutely continuous} with respect
to $\mu_U$ (i.e., $\mu(i)>0$ for every $i$ with $\mu_U(i)>0$, which on a
finite buffer collapses to $\mu(i)>0$ for all $i$), the IS identity holds:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_U(\theta) \;=\; \mathbb{E}_{i\sim\mu}\!\left[\frac{\mu_U(i)}{\mu(i)}\,
\ell(\delta_i(\theta))\right]
\;=\; \mathbb{E}_{i\sim\mu}\!\left[\frac{1}{N\,\mu(i)}\,\ell(\delta_i(\theta))\right],
\label{eq:is-identity}
\end{equation}
where the second equality substitutes $\mu_U(i)=1/N$.
The estimator is unbiased for $\mathcal{L}_U$ for any such $\mu$;
its variance is minimized when $\mu(i)\propto\ell(\delta_i)$
(the textbook zero-variance proposal), which motivates priority-by-error.

\paragraph{Step 3: PER as a special case and the annealing knob.}
Standard PER \citep{schaul2016per} chooses
$\mu_P(i)\propto(|\delta_i|+\varepsilon)^\alpha$ with priority floor
$\varepsilon\!>\!0$ (which keeps $\mu_P$ strictly positive and hence
absolutely continuous w.r.t.\ $\mu_U$), and replaces the IS exponent
$-1$ by an \emph{annealed} exponent $-\beta_t$ that interpolates
between uniform-weight ($\beta_0=0.4$) and exact correction ($\beta=1$):
\begin{equation}
\widehat{\mathcal{L}}_P(\theta) \;=\; \mathbb{E}_{i\sim\mu_P}\!\left[
\bigl(N\,\mu_P(i)\bigr)^{-\beta_t}\,\ell(\delta_i(\theta))
\right], \qquad \beta_t\colon \beta_0=0.4 \to 1.
\label{eq:per-anneal}
\end{equation}
Substituting $\beta_t\!=\!1$ into~\eqref{eq:per-anneal} recovers
\eqref{eq:is-identity} verbatim and so equals $\mathcal{L}_U$ exactly;
$\beta_t\!<\!1$ trades bias (toward the high-priority tail) for
variance, with the bias monotone in $1\!-\!\beta_t$. In implementation,
PER additionally rescales the IS weights by the per-batch maximum
$w_{\max}^{\text{batch}}\!=\!\max_{j\in\mathcal{B}}(N\mu_P(j))^{-\beta_t}$
as a numerical stabilizer (Schaul et al., Algorithm~1); this is a
self-normalization that leaves the estimand $\mathcal{L}_U$ unchanged in
expectation under stop-gradient on $\mu_P$ (Appendix~\ref{app:unbiased},
condition (A3)).

\paragraph{Step 4: Semantic PER as IS with a product proposal.}
Let $q_\phi(t^\star\!\mid\!\tau)$ be the VLM's posterior over the
outcome-causing timestep and $w_{\text{sem}}(i;\tau_i)$ the
per-transition semantic boost from Eq.~\eqref{eq:wsem}, with
$w_{\text{sem}}\!\in\![1,w_{\max}]$. The Semantic-PER proposal is the
normalized product
\begin{equation}
\mu_{\text{Sem}}(i) \;=\; \frac{\mu_P(i)\,w_{\text{sem}}(i;\tau_i)}{Z},
\qquad
Z \;=\; \sum_{j=1}^{N} \mu_P(j)\,w_{\text{sem}}(j;\tau_j),
\label{eq:sem-prop}
\end{equation}
where the floor $\varepsilon^\alpha\!>\!0$ on $\mu_P$ and the lower bound
$w_{\text{sem}}\!\ge\!1$ together imply $\mu_{\text{Sem}}(i)\!>\!0$ for
every slot, preserving absolute continuity w.r.t.\ $\mu_U$. Plugging
$\mu_{\text{Sem}}$ into~\eqref{eq:is-identity} gives the
\emph{strictly-corrected} Semantic-PER estimator:
\begin{equation}
\widehat{\mathcal{L}}_{\text{Sem}}(\theta) \;=\; \mathbb{E}_{i\sim\mu_{\text{Sem}}}\!\left[
\bigl(N\,\mu_{\text{Sem}}(i)\bigr)^{-\beta_t}\,\ell(\delta_i(\theta))\right].
\label{eq:sem-corrected}
\end{equation}
At $\beta_t\!=\!1$, expanding the inner factor yields
$(N\mu_{\text{Sem}}(i))^{-1}\ell(\delta_i) \cdot \mu_{\text{Sem}}(i)
=\ell(\delta_i)/N$ inside the implicit $\sum_i$ of the expectation,
so $\widehat{\mathcal{L}}_{\text{Sem}}$ equals $\mathcal{L}_U$ identically
(not merely in expectation). This is the precise sense in which
\eqref{eq:sem-corrected} is a ``principled multiplicative reweighting''
of \eqref{eq:headline}: the boost $w_{\text{sem}}$ enters the proposal
without altering the estimand. As $\beta_t\!\to\!1$, the
$w_{\text{sem}}$-induced concentration on the failure window is
exactly cancelled by the IS denominator; for $\beta_t\!<\!1$ that
cancellation is partial and the residual is Step~5's bias.

\paragraph{Step 5: the under-correction we actually run.}
Our running implementation uses the PER IS weight
$w_{\text{IS},P}(i)\!=\!(N\,\mu_P(i))^{-\beta_t}$ rather than the
strictly-correct $w_{\text{IS},\text{Sem}}(i)\!=\!(N\,\mu_{\text{Sem}}(i))^{-\beta_t}$.
The ratio at any slot is
$w_{\text{IS},P}/w_{\text{IS},\text{Sem}}=(\mu_{\text{Sem}}/\mu_P)^{\beta_t}
=(w_{\text{sem}}(i;\tau_i)/Z)^{\beta_t}$, where
$Z=\sum_j \mu_P(j)\,w_{\text{sem}}(j;\tau_j)\!\in\![1,w_{\max}]$ is the
$\mu_P$-weighted average semantic boost (using $\sum_j\mu_P(j)\!=\!1$).
Since $w_{\text{sem}}\!\ge\!1$ and $Z\!\le\!w_{\max}$ is bounded by the
same ceiling as the boost, semantic-boosted slots are systematically
under-corrected by a factor in $[1, w_{\max}^{\beta_t}]$.
The resulting estimator is biased toward emphasizing the failure
window---a controlled, computable bias-for-variance tradeoff that is
structurally analogous to V-trace's ratio truncation
(\S\ref{app:vtrace}); the per-step bound is given in
\S\ref{app:unbiased}, Eq.~\eqref{eq:bias-bound}. The
strictly-correct ablation (Eq.~\eqref{eq:sem-corrected} with
$\beta_t\!=\!1$ and $w_{\text{IS},\text{Sem}}$) has not been run
in this work and is the natural follow-on; the limitations
(\S\ref{sec:discussion}) flag this explicitly.

\paragraph{Scope of the derivation.}
Three things in Steps~1--5 are standard: (a) the IS identity
\eqref{eq:is-identity}; (b) the priority-by-error form of $\mu_P$ and
its annealed IS weight \eqref{eq:per-anneal}; (c) the bias--variance
character of $\beta_t\!<\!1$. Two things are specific to this paper:
(d) the identification of a frozen VLM's posterior as the
trajectory-conditional modifier $w_{\text{sem}}$, which keeps the
IS correction \emph{tractable} because $w_{\text{sem}}$ is
$\theta$-independent (Appendix~\ref{app:unbiased}, (A3)) and bounded
(A1); (e) the resulting bias of the under-corrected variant
\eqref{eq:bias-bound}, whose two-regime degradation behavior under
$q_\phi$-miscalibration is given in
Appendix~\ref{app:unbiased}'s closing paragraphs and separated cleanly
from the verified-CF channel's signal-extinction regime
(\S\ref{sec:method:verified}; \S\ref{sec:discussion} bullet (ix)).
Items~(d)--(e) are where the framing earns its place beyond a textbook
IS application.

\subsection{Connection to V-trace and Retrace}
\label{app:vtrace}

V-trace \citep{espeholt2018impala} and Retrace($\lambda$)
\citep{munos2016retrace} are off-policy correction schemes for
\emph{multi-step temporal-difference returns} with the proposal
being the behavior policy. They specialize as follows.
The key structural concept shared by all three methods is the
\emph{IS-pairing requirement}: the IS weight used in the gradient
estimate must be paired to the proposal that generated the data.
Changing the proposal without changing the weight --- or vice versa ---
implicitly retargets the loss and breaks the corrected-estimator
interpretation. V-trace, Retrace, and Semantic PER each enforce IS-pairing
via a truncation or under-correction whose magnitude is controlled by a
single scalar parameter ($\bar\rho$, $\lambda$, and $w_{\max}$
respectively); in each case the parameter governs a bias--variance
tradeoff with the same signed effect (lower value = more bias toward
the truncated tail, less variance).

\paragraph{Specialization of V-trace.}
V-trace defines per-step IS ratios $\rho_t = \pi(a_t\!\mid\!s_t)/\mu_b(a_t\!\mid\!s_t)$
and a target-policy product $c_t = \min(\bar c, \rho_t)$. The N-step
target is
$v_s = V(s) + \sum_{t\ge s}\gamma^{t-s}\bigl(\prod_{t'<t}c_{t'}\bigr) \min(\bar\rho,\rho_t)\,
\delta_t$. When $q_\phi$ is uniform on $\{0,\dots,T-1\}$, $w_{\text{sem}}\equiv 1$
and our scheme degenerates to PER --- which is single-step
($N\!=\!1$ in V-trace), with $\mu=\mu_P$ rather than $\mu_b$, and with
the IS weight $w_{\text{IS}}=(|\mathcal{D}_B|\mu_P)^{-\beta_t}$ playing
the role of V-trace's $\rho_t$.
The structural mapping between the two truncation mechanisms is as follows.
V-trace clips $\rho_t$ to $\bar\rho$; our under-correction clips the
effective IS ratio $\mu_{\text{Sem}}/\mu_P = w_{\text{sem}}/Z$ to
$w_{\max}/Z\!\le\! w_{\max}$. In V-trace, Espeholt et al.\ (2018, Lemma~1)
bound the per-step bias introduced by the $\bar\rho$ truncation: the
bias grows with $\bar\rho$ and with the policy gap $|\pi-\mu_b|$.
In our setting the analogous bound is Eq.~\eqref{eq:bias-bound}: the
bias grows with $w_{\max}^{\beta_t}$ (the maximum ratio truncation) and
with the fractional window size $(2W+1)/T$ (the analog of policy gap ---
how concentrated the proposal is on the boosted region). The two bounds
share the form $[\text{truncation level}]\times[\text{concentration of
proposal}]\times\|\ell\|_\infty$; in V-trace the first factor is $\bar\rho$
and the second is a horizon-discounted policy-gap norm; in Semantic PER
the first factor is $w_{\max}^{\beta_t}-1$ and the second is
$(2W+1)/T$.

\paragraph{Specialization of Retrace.}
Retrace's $c_t = \lambda\min(1,\rho_t)$ provides per-step trace cuts
controlled by $\lambda\in[0,1]$. The IS-pairing requirement for Retrace
is that the per-step clipped ratio $c_t$ is derived from the \emph{same}
behavior policy $\mu_b$ that generated the trajectory; using a different
weight function (e.g., clipping to a constant unrelated to $\rho_t$)
would break the Retrace guarantee. Setting $\lambda=0$ reduces Retrace to
1-step Q-learning, discarding all IS adjustment. Setting $\lambda=1$
recovers the IS-corrected multi-step return (subject to the $\min(1,\cdot)$
variance cap). In Semantic PER terms, the $\lambda$ knob in Retrace is
analogous to the $\beta_t$ anneal: $\lambda=0$ (no correction) corresponds
to $\beta_t=0$ (uniform weights), and $\lambda=1$ (full clipped correction)
corresponds to $\beta_t=1$ (full IS correction), with the key difference
that Retrace's truncation ceiling is fixed at $1$ while Semantic PER's
ceiling grows with $w_{\max}^{\beta_t}$. In both cases violating the
IS-pairing requirement --- using a weight not derived from the generating
proposal --- introduces an uncontrolled bias; the design choice in Semantic
PER to use $\mu_P$-derived weights rather than $\mu_{\text{Sem}}$-derived
weights is precisely such a violation, acknowledged in Step~5 of
\S\ref{app:is-derivation} and bounded by Eq.~\eqref{eq:bias-bound}.

\paragraph{Why the analogy is loose.}
V-trace and Retrace correct for \emph{policy mismatch} in multi-step
returns from a behavior trajectory. Semantic PER corrects for
\emph{replay-distribution mismatch} from a posterior-weighted proposal.
The proposals being corrected are different objects: V-trace's $\mu_b$
is the behavior policy at action selection; our $\mu_{\text{Sem}}$ is
the per-slot sampling distribution at minibatch construction. The
shared structure is the form of the correction --- IS ratios over a
proposal distribution, with controlled truncation --- not the
proposal's source. In particular, the bias bounds live in different
problem geometries: V-trace's bound (Espeholt 2018, Lemma~1) depends
on trajectory length and policy-gap norm; ours (Eq.~\eqref{eq:bias-bound})
depends on window fraction and per-batch max loss. Neither bound implies
the other, and the V-trace analogy should be read as a structural
parallel in how truncation controls bias, not as a mathematical
specialization in either direction.

\subsection{Conditions for unbiasedness}
\label{app:unbiased}

The semantic-PER estimator (Eq.~\ref{eq:sem-corrected}) is unbiased for
$\mathcal{L}_U$ when:

\begin{enumerate}\itemsep1pt
\item[(A1)] \textbf{Bounded boost.} $w_{\text{sem}}(i;\tau_i)\in[1,w_{\max}]$
with $w_{\max}<\infty$. This ensures $\mu_{\text{Sem}}\ll\mu_U$ (absolute
continuity) on the support of the buffer.
\item[(A2)] \textbf{Strict IS correction.} $\beta_t=1$ is used in
Equation~\eqref{eq:sem-corrected} (rather than the annealed value). For
$\beta_t<1$ the estimator is biased; the bias decays as $\beta_t\to 1$.
\item[(A3)] \textbf{Stop-gradient on the proposal.} The proposal
$\mu_{\text{Sem}}(i)=\mu_P(i)\cdot w_{\text{sem}}(i;\tau_i)/Z$ is
treated as a \emph{fixed} sampling weight when computing the loss gradient:
$\partial\mu_{\text{Sem}}/\partial\theta=0$. This stop-gradient
is the standard practice in PER \citep{schaul2016per} and is equivalent
to requiring that the gradient $\nabla_\theta\mathcal{L}$ is estimated
under $\mu_{\text{Sem}}$ held fixed at its current value.
Note: $\mu_P(i)\propto(|\delta_i(\theta)|+\varepsilon)^\alpha$ does depend
on the current critic $\theta$ through $\delta_i$, but the stop-gradient
operator (which practical PER applies by detaching the sampling distribution
from the computational graph) makes this dependence irrelevant for the IS
interpretation. The frozen-VLM property of $q_\phi$ (a function of rendered
keyframes, not of $\theta$) ensures that $w_{\text{sem}}$ is itself
$\theta$-independent; the $\theta$-dependence of $\mu_{\text{Sem}}$ enters
only through $\mu_P$, which is stopped.
\item[(A4)] \textbf{Buffer full-support.} $\mu_{\text{Sem}}(i)>0$ for
every $i\in\{0,\dots,|\mathcal{D}_B|-1\}$. Since $p_i=(|\delta_i|+\varepsilon)^\alpha
\cdot w_i \ge \varepsilon^\alpha>0$ by construction (priority floor $\varepsilon>0$
and $w_i\ge 1$), this is satisfied for every buffer configuration.
\item[(A5)] \textbf{Approximate calibration.}
$\|q_\phi(\cdot\!\mid\!\tau) - p_{\text{true}}(\cdot\!\mid\!\tau)\|_{\text{TV}}
\le \varepsilon_{\text{cal}}$ for some $\varepsilon_{\text{cal}}\in[0,1]$.
This condition bounds the variance amplification due to VLM miscalibration.
When $\varepsilon_{\text{cal}}=0$ (perfect calibration) the IS variance
is minimized; when $\varepsilon_{\text{cal}}=1$ the VLM is ignored
($w_{\text{sem}}\equiv 1$ in expectation) and we recover PER variance.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Unbiasedness and miscalibration for the strictly-corrected estimator.}
For Eq.~\eqref{eq:sem-corrected} with $\beta_t=1$ (the strictly-corrected
variant), (A1)--(A4) are sufficient for unbiasedness: the IS ratio
$\mu_U/\mu_{\text{Sem}} = (|\mathcal{D}_B|\,\mu_{\text{Sem}})^{-1}$ is a
valid IS weight, and (A4) ensures the ratio is finite everywhere.
In this regime miscalibration of $q_\phi$ (assumption A5 with $\varepsilon_{\text{cal}}>0$)
affects \emph{variance} but not bias: any non-zero proposal with $\mu_{\text{Sem}}\ll\mu_U$
yields an unbiased estimator of $\mathcal{L}_U$, with variance inversely proportional
to the quality of the proposal.

\paragraph{Bias of the implemented under-corrected estimator.}
Our running implementation uses $w_{\text{IS},P}(i)=(|\mathcal{D}_B|\,\mu_P(i))^{-\beta_t}$
rather than $w_{\text{IS},\text{Sem}}(i)=(|\mathcal{D}_B|\,\mu_{\text{Sem}}(i))^{-\beta_t}$.
The ratio between the two IS weights at any slot $i$ is:
\[
  \frac{w_{\text{IS},P}(i)}{w_{\text{IS},\text{Sem}}(i)}
  \;=\;
  \left(\frac{\mu_{\text{Sem}}(i)}{\mu_P(i)}\right)^{\beta_t}
  \;=\;
  \bigl(w_{\text{sem}}(i;\tau_i)\bigr)^{\beta_t}\,
  \Bigl(\sum_j \mu_P(j)/\text{Z}\Bigr)^{-\beta_t},
\]
where $Z$ is the normalizing constant of $\mu_{\text{Sem}}$.
The per-slot bias of the implemented estimator relative to $\mathcal{L}_U$ is
bounded:
\begin{equation}
  \bigl|\widehat{\mathcal{L}}_{\text{under}} - \mathcal{L}_U\bigr|
  \;\le\;
  C\,\bigl(w_{\max}^{\,\beta_t} - 1\bigr)\,\frac{2W+1}{T}
  \;\|\ell\|_\infty,
  \label{eq:bias-bound}
\end{equation}
where $C$ is a constant depending on $|\mathcal{D}_B|$ and the PER normalization,
$w_{\max}^{\,\beta_t}-1$ measures the under-correction strength (goes to 0 at
$w_{\max}=1$ or $\beta_t=0$, and is maximized at $w_{\max}\!=\!10$, $\beta_t\!=\!1$),
and $(2W+1)/T=11/50\approx 0.22$ is the fractional window size at our defaults.
At the end of training ($\beta_t\to 1$), the bias is largest; the strictly-correct
ablation (run $w_{\text{IS},\text{Sem}}$) removes this bias entirely at the
cost of maintaining the $\mu_{\text{Sem}}$ normalization constant.
Under (A5) with $\varepsilon_{\text{cal}}>0$, the implemented estimator's bias
also has a $q_\phi$-dependent component: when $q_\phi$ concentrates on a wrong
$t^\star$, the window boost amplifies the wrong slots, and the under-correction
amplifies that effect rather than compensating for it. The honest framing is
therefore: \emph{the implemented Semantic PER is a non-uniform replay scheme
whose bias is bounded by Eq.~\eqref{eq:bias-bound} and controlled by
$(w_{\max}, W, \beta_t)$; it delivers an unbiased estimator in the limit
$\beta_t\to 1$ with $w_{\text{IS},\text{Sem}}$, deferred to a future ablation.}

\paragraph{Two regimes of $q_\phi$-degeneracy: variance amplification vs.\ signal extinction.}
The bias bound \eqref{eq:bias-bound} and the verifier-gate analysis of
\S\ref{sec:method:verified} use $q_\phi$ in two operationally distinct
roles, and the conditions under which $q_\phi$ degrades affect them
asymmetrically. We make this asymmetry precise here because it
underwrites both the multiplicative-form argument of \S\ref{sec:theory}
and the cold-start caveat of \S\ref{sec:discussion}, bullet (ix).

\emph{Decomposition of $q_\phi$ on the verifier channel.}
For the verified-CF stack, write the effective marginal mass that
$q_\phi$ contributes to a relabel write on a failed episode $\tau$ as
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  m_\phi(\tau) \;=\;& \underbrace{\Pr_{q_\phi}\!\bigl[\text{VLM emits a parseable action 4-vector}\bigr]}_{m_{\text{gen}}} \\
  &\cdot\;
  \underbrace{\Pr_{\mu_b,\,\text{sim}}\!\bigl[r_t\!\ge\!0\text{ at some }t\!\le\!N\,\big|\,\sigma,\,a_{K:K+N}\!\sim\!q_\phi\bigr]}_{m_{\text{ver}}(\sigma)},
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:verifier-mass}
\end{equation}
where $m_{\text{gen}}\!\in\![0,1]$ is the VLM's parse rate and
$m_{\text{ver}}(\sigma)\!\in\![0,1]$ is the verifier-acceptance rate
conditional on snapshot state $\sigma\!=\!s_K$. Equation~\eqref{eq:verifier-mass}
factors a property of the generator from a property of the snapshot
distribution. The accepted-CF buffer measure $\nu_{\text{vcf}}$ is then
absolutely continuous w.r.t.\ $q_\phi$ on the support
$\{m_\phi>0\}$, with no mass elsewhere by construction
(rejected proposals are not written; cf.\ Fig.~\ref{fig:protocol},
step~5).

\emph{Cold-start as $m_{\text{ver}}\!\to\!0$ with $m_{\text{gen}}$ bounded away from zero.}
The cold-start regime documented in \S\ref{sec:discussion}, bullet (ix)
(101/101 rejections at step $\sim$143k on
\texttt{path\_c\_vlm\_vcf\_pp\_s42}; cf.\ bake-off mass concentrating
on teleport-like outputs that fail the action-vector parse only on
parse-strict variants and otherwise fail at step~(4)) is the regime
$m_{\text{ver}}(\sigma)\!\to\!0$ uniformly over the early-training
snapshot distribution, even when $m_{\text{gen}}$ is well above zero.
By Eq.~\eqref{eq:verifier-mass}, $m_\phi\!\to\!0$, so
$\nu_{\text{vcf}}$ is the zero measure on the buffer for the cold-start
duration. The verifier's $\varepsilon_{\text{cal}}\!=\!0$ guarantee
(A5 specialized to the accepted set, \S\ref{sec:method:verified})
holds vacuously: every element of the empty set has perfect
calibration. The bias bound \eqref{eq:bias-bound} also holds
vacuously for the VCF channel during this regime, but at the
operational cost that the VCF channel contributes zero new TD signal;
the run reduces to a strictly costlier HER (\S\ref{sec:discussion}
bullet (ix); cf.\ also the Phase~2 Oracle-CF cross-seed variance
discussion in \S\ref{sec:exp:inflight}, where the same mechanism
predicts the high-variance, two-mode seed distribution we observe
empirically). The theoretically clean statement is:
\emph{the verifier converts $q_\phi$-miscalibration into a
write-throughput bottleneck rather than into a biased buffer write,
and the cold-start regime is the limiting case in which the
throughput is zero.}

\emph{Semantic PER is non-degenerate under the same condition.}
The Semantic-PER channel uses $q_\phi$ as a multiplicative
proposal modifier on \emph{every} buffer slot
(Eq.~\eqref{eq:sem-prop}); its proposal $\mu_{\text{Sem}}$ inherits
$\mu_P$'s full support on $\mathcal{D}_B$ by (A1) and (A4), independently
of whether any individual VLM call would survive a verifier gate. In
particular, $\mu_{\text{Sem}}(i)\ge\varepsilon^\alpha\!\cdot\!1/Z\!>\!0$
for every slot even when $q_\phi$ is maximally miscalibrated
($\varepsilon_{\text{cal}}\!=\!1$ in A5), because the lower bound
$w_{\text{sem}}\!\ge\!1$ in (A1) prevents semantic priors from
extinguishing slots. The cold-start condition
$m_{\text{ver}}(\sigma)\!\to\!0$, which would close the VCF channel,
inflates the bias of the implemented Semantic-PER estimator only
through the (A5) variance-amplification term --- bounded by
Eq.~\eqref{eq:bias-bound} and not by any feasibility condition on
$q_\phi$. The two regimes are therefore: \emph{(i)~Semantic PER
$q_\phi$-degeneracy is variance amplification, bounded;
(ii)~Verified-CF $q_\phi$-degeneracy is signal extinction,
unbounded in the limit $m_{\text{ver}}\!\to\!0$.} This is the
formal version of the throughput-vs.-quality-dial framing in
\S\ref{sec:method:verified}: the two pathways do not fail in
mathematically isomorphic ways under the same miscalibration
stressor, which is the principal reason the paper treats them as
complementary contributions and audits them separately in
\S\ref{sec:exp} rather than averaging their outcomes.

\noindent\textbf{Note on HER$\,+\,$Semantic-PER interaction.}
When HER inserts a hindsight-relabeled copy of slot~$i$ (with goal $g'\!\neq\!g$) into the buffer, it occupies a \emph{new} ring-buffer slot $i'$ whose semantic weight is initialized to the same $w_i$ as the originating transition (line~9 of Algorithm~\ref{alg:sem-per}).  The practical effect is that the failure-window boost is inherited by HER copies of failure-window transitions.  The IS correction at line~13 uses the ring-buffer slot count $|\mathcal{D}_B|\!=\!10^6$ (a constant throughout training, since the buffer is pre-allocated) regardless of how many slots contain HER vs.\ real transitions; at steady state approximately $80\%$ of occupied slots are HER copies.  The worst-case effective boost for a failure-window slot is therefore $w_{\max}\!=\!10$ (unchanged), because both the real transition and its 4 HER copies are sampled from the same sum-tree and each has the same leaf priority $p_i\!=\!(|\delta_i|+\varepsilon)^\alpha\!\cdot\!w_{\text{sem}}$.  The number of semantically-boosted \emph{slots} increases by the HER multiplier (1 real $+$ 4 HER $=$ 5 slots per failure-window timestep), but the per-slot boost factor is unchanged.  We note this interaction explicitly here for reimplementers.

\subsection{Random seeds and statistical methodology}
\label{app:stats}

\paragraph{What counts as a ``seed''.}
A seed is a single non-negative integer used to instantiate (i)
\texttt{numpy.random.default\_rng}, (ii)
\texttt{torch.manual\_seed}, (iii) \texttt{torch.cuda.manual\_seed\_all},
(iv) Gymnasium-Robotics \texttt{env.reset(seed=...)}, and (v) any
\texttt{random.Random} instances used by the trainer. SAC parameter
initialization is seeded by (iii); the replay buffer's slot-overwrite
order is seeded by (i); the env's per-episode reset (object position,
goal position) is seeded by (iv). Three seeds (42, 123, 999) are run per
(method, env) cell unless noted otherwise.

\paragraph{Confidence intervals.}
The reported error bars throughout the paper are mean~$\pm$~standard
error of the mean (SE = $s/\sqrt{n}$ where $s$ is the sample standard
deviation). For $n=3$ this is wide and only directional; we say so in
the figure captions. The cross-task transfer pilot
(Section~\ref{sec:exp:transfer}) reports raw fractions at $n=12$; a
12/12 binary fraction is not statistically distinguishable from an 80\%
underlying rate without a larger sample. Prompt-design metrics in
Table~\ref{tab:prompt-variants} are pooled across $\le\!6$ episodes per
cell; we report SE rather than bootstrap CIs because the per-episode
spread dominates and a bootstrap at $n=6$ underestimates uncertainty.

\paragraph{Bootstrap protocol (when used).}
Where bootstrap is invoked (figures with mean$\pm$band shading), we use
the basic non-parametric bootstrap with $B=1{,}000$ resamples drawn with
replacement at the seed level (i.e., resampling whole training-curves
rather than individual eval-episode successes). The reported band is
the 16th--84th percentile range, corresponding approximately to
$\pm 1\,\mathrm{SE}$ under a normal approximation.

\subsection{Evaluation protocol}
\label{app:eval}

\paragraph{Eval schedule.}
Eval is triggered every $5{,}000$ env steps (\texttt{eval\_interval}).
Each eval comprises $E\!=\!20$ episodes with the policy in deterministic
mode (action $=$ tanh of actor's mean output, no entropy bonus). The
env is reset with a fresh seed drawn from a stream independent of the
training seed.

\paragraph{Success criterion.}
An eval episode is a success iff $\|\text{ag}_T - g\|<0.05$~m at the
final step. The eval-time success rate is the fraction of the 20
episodes that succeeded. Note that Fetch's training reward is
$r=-\mathbb{1}[\|\text{ag}_t-g\|>0.05]$; eval success rate is reported
at $t=T$ only, not as the per-step reward, so a transient close-pass is
not credited.

\paragraph{Checkpoint frequency.}
Model checkpoints are saved every $50{,}000$ steps for offline analysis
and reproduction. Eval is logged independently (no model save at eval
time).

\paragraph{Final reporting metric.}
The reported headline metric is success rate at the \emph{final
checkpoint} (i.e., the eval-time success at the end of training for
each run). The plotted curves in Figure~\ref{fig:headline} show the
training-time trajectory of this metric, mean$\pm$SE across the three
seeds, with horizontal axis env-steps.

\subsection{Broader Impacts}
\label{app:impact}

Section~\ref{sec:discussion} (``Broader impact'') gives the
high-level positive/negative/scope structure for the main body. We
expand here on five lower-level questions that the main paragraph
elides for length: foundation-model access asymmetry and
reproducibility-equity, the net carbon accounting of trading env
steps for VLM calls, the dual-use surface of the generator--verifier
pattern, an explicit \emph{What this work is not} scope statement, and
the safety properties of training-time-only foundation-model use. The
purpose of the longer treatment is to surface considerations that a
NeurIPS Broader-Impacts reader can use to evaluate the work without
having to derive them from the methodological body.

\paragraph{Reproducibility-equity and foundation-model access
asymmetry.}
The two leading VLMs we evaluate (Claude Opus~4.7 and GPT-4o) are
closed-API products available only through paid services from
Anthropic and OpenAI respectively. Replicating the full paper-grade
sweep costs $\approx\!\$320$ in inference fees
(Appendix~\ref{app:compute}); the in-flight verified-CF sweep alone
is $\approx\!\$85$ per run. This places a non-trivial floor on the
researcher cohort that can reproduce or extend the method as
specified, and is a material reproducibility-equity concern that
\emph{open-data} reproducibility checklists alone do not surface.
Three structural mitigations are built into the codebase release.
\emph{(a)~Heuristic-Oracle fallback.} The Oracle v3 heuristic
(Appendix~\ref{app:oracle-v3}) provides a fully VLM-free credit-assignment
oracle that uses only state-geometry and the env's own reward
function. It runs on CPU, requires no API key, and reproduces the
mechanism (PER prior $\times$ semantic boost) at zero
foundation-model cost. The headline Path~C kill experiment was decided
against Oracle-CF at the $250\,\mathrm{k}$-step horizon, so the
load-bearing decision in the paper does not require API access to
replicate.
\emph{(b)~Open-weights VLM extension is pre-registered.} We
pre-register an open-weights extension (LLaVA-NeXT, Qwen2.5-VL-7B,
Pixtral) in \S\ref{sec:exp:real} as the natural robustness
follow-on. These models run on a single 16~GB consumer GPU and
require zero per-call API cost; the published commit includes a
shim layer in \texttt{src/vlm/} so that the same prompt template
addresses an open-weights endpoint without code change.
\emph{(c)~Per-call cost and rate-limit reporting.} The codebase
release ships with a \texttt{COST.md} that lists per-call token-count
and dollar-cost on a representative sweep, so a reader contemplating
a replication can compute an a-priori budget without having to run
the pipeline. The same file is the basis of the
Appendix~\ref{app:compute} dollar figures.

\paragraph{Net carbon accounting: env steps traded for VLM calls.}
Sample-efficient methods reduce GPU-hours by reducing env-step count,
but VLM inference is itself an energy expenditure on a distant data
center. The net carbon delta of trading env steps for VLM calls is
not a priori positive. For our scheme the accounting is:
$1.7\!\times\!10^3$ VLM calls per $500\,\mathrm{k}$-step run
(Appendix~\ref{app:compute}; one call per failed episode at the
$\texttt{vlm\_call\_interval}\!=\!16$ default), each
$\le\!\$0.05$ and $\sim\!8$~kJ of inference energy on
GPT-4o's reported tokens-per-Joule profile, vs.\ a $500\,\mathrm{k}$-step
SAC run consuming $\sim\!3.5$~GPU-hours on A10G ($\approx\!2$~MJ at
$150\,\mathrm{W}$). VLM inference is therefore $\sim\!10^{-2}$ of the
training run's energy at the call rate we use; the sample-efficiency
gain dominates. The conclusion would invert if either (i) the
call-rate budget were raised by two orders of magnitude (e.g., one
VLM call per env-step rather than per failed episode), or (ii) the
training run were itself short ($\le\!50\,\mathrm{k}$ steps), at which
point the cross-over point is reached. We document this so future
work scaling the call rate has a numeric basis for the trade-off.

\paragraph{Dual-use surface of the generator--verifier pattern.}
The verified-CF acceptance gate is a general-purpose pattern: a
foundation-model generator produces a low-bandwidth structured
proposal (here, a 4-vector action), and a deterministic verifier
either accepts or rejects based on a substrate test (here, MuJoCo
sparse reward firing). The pattern transfers naturally to
program-synthesis-with-tests \citep{zha2025tango}, constraint
satisfaction with SMT verifiers, and lemma-suggestion in interactive
theorem proving. The dual-use surface comes from the same structural
strength: a generator that learns to \emph{satisfy the verifier
without satisfying the underlying intent} can be a vector for
specification gaming. In robotics this is bounded by the env's
sparse-reward predicate (the verifier accepts only physically-correct
goal achievement), but in domains where the verifier is itself
learned, partial, or game-able (e.g., proof-checking with axiom
schemas the prover can introduce, or test-suite coverage that the
generator can adversarially target), the same accept/reject design
admits a Goodhart's-law failure mode that this paper's setting does
not exhibit. We flag this because the methodological recipe will
travel ahead of its physics-grounded verifier; readers transplanting
the pattern to non-physics domains should explicitly audit whether
the verifier is closed under the generator's output distribution or
can be elicited to fire on outputs that fail the original objective.

\paragraph{Safety properties of training-time-only foundation-model
use.}
A structural property of our scheme worth surfacing: the foundation
model is invoked only at \emph{training time}, only on offline replay
data, and only to influence the buffer's sampling distribution (Semantic
PER) or to propose a candidate relabel that is then verified by the env
itself (Verified~CF). The deployed policy is a standard SAC actor
network; it carries no VLM weights, makes no foundation-model API
call at inference, and has no dependence on the foundation-model
provider's API uptime or behavior. This compares favorably to recent
VLM-as-policy and VLM-as-reward lines
\citep{rocamonde2024vlmreward,wu2026largerewardmodels}: a VLM
specification drift, API deprecation, or adversarial-prompt incident
after training has no effect on the trained agent's runtime behavior.
The reproducibility implication is also positive: re-running the
training pipeline several months later against a different VLM
checkpoint produces a different sampling distribution, but the
trained policy is a function of the env's true sparse reward and the
SAC objective, both of which are stable. The implication for
deployment audits is that a regulator inspecting the agent's runtime
behavior does not need access to the VLM that was used during
training.

\paragraph{What this work is not.}
We make four explicit non-claims, since each has been a common
mis-reading pattern in adjacent VLM-in-RL work and we want the
broader-impacts reader to have an unambiguous scope statement.
\emph{(N1)}~This is not a sim-to-real claim. All experiments are
simulation-only on three Gymnasium-Robotics Fetch envs. The
heuristic and verifier both depend on a forkable, sparse-reward
training simulator, which is structurally unavailable in real-robot
training. Section~\ref{sec:discussion} bullet~(vi) discusses what
would be required to extend.
\emph{(N2)}~This is not a foundation-model evaluation paper. We do
not benchmark VLMs against each other on a fixed VLM eval suite;
when a sub-claim does require a model comparison
(\S\ref{sec:exp:prompt}, \S\ref{sec:exp:real}) we report it on
$n\!\le\!10$ episodes per cell with disjoint Clopper--Pearson
intervals, and we are explicit that the load-bearing finding is
\emph{prompt-architectural rather than vendor-specific}.
\emph{(N3)}~This is not a multi-agent or human-interaction system.
The VLM is invoked offline on replay frames; it does not interact
with a human user during training, does not generate natural-language
output that is shown to a deployer, and does not act as a teacher
in a teacher--student RL loop.
\emph{(N4)}~This is not an autonomy claim. The agent we train is a
single-task goal-conditioned policy with a 50-step horizon; we make
no claim about open-ended task selection, multi-step planning, or
self-improvement.

\paragraph{Personal data, consent, and content moderation.}
No human-subjects data, identifiable images, or personal text are
collected, generated, or processed at any pipeline stage. The
prompt corpus consists of rendered MuJoCo frames of simulated
gripper-and-block scenes, which contain no faces, text, license
plates, or identifiable scene content. The VLM API providers'
standard content policies apply to our calls, but no policy-violating
content was ever generated or attempted; we filed no abuse reports
and received none. The codebase release contains no copyrighted
images, no scraped web content, and no human-annotated labels.

\section{Failure Mode Analysis}
\label{app:failures}

\subsection{The teleport-collapse failure mode (C1v2-A)}
\label{app:teleport}

\paragraph{Formal characterization.}
The teleport-collapse failure mode of VLM counterfactual prompting is
the event $\|p_{\text{cf}}-g\|<\rho$ where $p_{\text{cf}}$ is the VLM's
emitted \texttt{corrective\_position} for a failed episode and $g$ is
the desired-goal of that episode (with $\rho\!=\!0.05$~m). Empirically,
this mode appears in:
\begin{itemize}\itemsep0pt
\item Claude Opus 4.7, FetchPush, \textsc{achieved\_goal}: $4/4$ episodes (100\%).
\item Claude Opus 4.7, FetchPickAndPlace, \textsc{achieved\_goal}: $3/4$ episodes on real, $2/2$ on synthetic.
\item GPT-4o, FetchPush, \textsc{achieved\_goal}: $0/6$ on synthetic;
on the joint \textsc{all} variant: $4/6$ on synthetic.
\item Claude Sonnet 4.5, FetchPush, \textsc{achieved\_goal}: $3/4$ on real (75\%).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Why it happens.}
The \textsc{achieved\_goal} prompt asks for ``the position the object
should have reached at the failure frame for the episode to be on
track''. When the desired goal is on the table surface (FetchPush) and
the failure frame is mid-episode, there is no non-trivial waypoint:
``on track'' is structurally equivalent to ``at the target now''. The
VLM resolves this ambiguity by output $p_{\text{cf}}=g$ exactly. On
PickAndPlace, the goal is in mid-air ($z>0.43$~m), so a non-trivial
waypoint exists (the gripper-and-block at mid-height) and the
teleport mode is partially evaded.

\paragraph{Mitigations evaluated.}
\begin{enumerate}\itemsep0pt
\item \textbf{Hard rejection gate.} Reject all outputs with
$\|p_{\text{cf}}-g\|<\rho$. Effective at gate-time but loses signal
($\sim$100\% of FetchPush \textsc{achieved\_goal} outputs rejected for
Claude Opus 4.7). Adopted as a belt-and-suspenders fallback in the
production pipeline.
\item \textbf{Prompt-variant switch to \textsc{all}.} The unified
\textsc{all} prompt structurally regularizes the position field (must
co-emit a position \emph{and} a 4-D action), reducing teleport rate
on real data from 100\% to 17--25\%. Helpful but not eliminative.
\item \textbf{Model switch.} Switching Opus~4.7 to Sonnet~4.5
rescues PickAndPlace (75\% $\to$ 0\%) but not Push (75\% persists).
Switching to GPT-4o eliminates Push on synthetic (100\% $\to$ 0\%)
but persists on the joint \textsc{all} variant.
\item \textbf{Mechanism replacement (verified-CF).} Switch to the
\textsc{action} variant + simulator-fork verification
(Algorithm~\ref{alg:verified-cf}). Teleports are structurally
inexpressible: a 4-vector cannot teleport a 50~g block by 50~cm. This is
the adopted mechanism.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Adopted production configuration.}
\texttt{(GPT-4o, \textsc{achieved\_goal} with $\rho\!=\!0.05$ gate,
verified-CF fallback)}. The combination of (i) GPT-4o's strict
dominance on the synthetic \textsc{achieved\_goal} grid, (ii) the gate
catching residual teleports, and (iii) the verified-CF mechanism
absorbing rejections gives a defense-in-depth strategy.

\subsection{Action sign-flip in real-data evaluation (C1v2-B)}
\label{app:sign-flip}

\paragraph{Phenomenon.}
On the \textsc{action} prompt variant, $\sim\!50$\% of VLM-emitted
4-vectors have at least one axis whose sign disagrees with
$\mathrm{sign}(g - \text{ag}_K)$ for the failure-frame achieved goal.
The VLM's textual explanation \emph{correctly} describes the failure
mode in $4/4$ episodes (e.g., ``descend and close the gripper''), but
the emitted action vector points in the wrong axis-direction. The judge
catches each contradiction with phrasing like ``the action's negative-y
component moves away from the target''.

\paragraph{When it occurs.}
The sign-flip rate per (source, model, variant):

\begin{itemize}\itemsep0pt
\item Synthetic, Opus 4.7, \textsc{action}: 0.50 ($n\!=\!4$).
\item Synthetic, Opus 4.7, \textsc{all}: 0.17 ($n\!=\!2$).
\item Real, Opus 4.7, \textsc{action}: 0.55 ($n\!=\!10$).
\item Real, Sonnet 4.5, \textsc{action}: 0.70 ($n\!=\!5$).
\item Real, Opus 4.7, \textsc{all}: 0.39 ($n\!=\!9$).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Hypothesized cause.}
VLMs have known difficulty grounding metric 3-D axis directions
(``which way is +y'') from 2-D rendered keyframes. The Fetch envs use
an angled tabletop camera; the y-axis appears as a horizontal
in-plane axis with no visual anchor, and the VLM frequently confuses
its sign. The \textsc{all} variant reduces this rate by forcing the VLM
to co-emit a position field (which it tends to get right) alongside
the action, providing implicit cross-checking.

\paragraph{Recommended sign-check gate.}
The recommended mitigation for any pipeline consuming \textsc{action}
outputs is the simple gate
\begin{equation}
\text{accept iff } \mathrm{sign}(a_{\text{cf}}[:\!3]) = \mathrm{sign}(g - \text{ag}_K)
\quad \text{component-wise on axes with } |g - \text{ag}_K| > 0.01~\mathrm{m}.
\label{eq:sign-gate}
\end{equation}
Empirically this filters $\approx 50$\% of \textsc{action} outputs.
The remaining $\sim\!50$\% have correct signs and are forwarded to
either the verified-CF mechanism or direct action-relabeling.

\paragraph{Why the verified-CF mechanism subsumes this.}
The simulator-fork verification (Algorithm~\ref{alg:verified-cf}) is
\emph{strictly stronger} than the sign-check gate: an
$a_{\text{cf}}$ with a flipped sign on any axis points the gripper
away from the goal, so the verifier env rolls out for 50~steps without
firing the sparse reward, and the counterfactual is rejected as
\texttt{goal\_not\_reached}. The sign-check gate is therefore mostly a
diagnostic and a pre-filter to save the verifier's 17.6\,ms per call;
on the production pipeline the verifier alone suffices.

\subsection{VLM API rate-limit at high parallelism (C1v2-C)}
\label{app:ratelimit}

\paragraph{Phenomenon.}
When running 18 parallel SAC+HER+Verified-CF seeds on Modal A10G
instances with \texttt{vlm\_call\_interval}=1 (every failed episode)
and model \texttt{claude-sonnet-4-5}, the Verified-CF mechanism
silently degrades: $73$\% of VLM calls return \texttt{None} after
retry exhaustion rather than a usable counterfactual.
Modal logs confirm the cause is HTTP~429 errors from Anthropic's
Tier~1 rate limits ($5$~req/min and $10{,}000$~tokens/min for
\texttt{claude-sonnet-4-5}).  The burst arrival rate of
$18~\text{seeds} \times \sim 1~\text{call/episode-end}$ easily
saturates both ceilings; the retry logic exhausts its budget
and increments the \texttt{buffer/cf\_vlm\_returned\_none} counter
rather than raising an exception, so the degradation is invisible
without explicit monitoring.

\paragraph{Why this is structural, not method-specific.}
Any VLM-in-replay scheme in which VLM calls are triggered
\emph{per failed episode} has a call rate that scales linearly
with the number of parallel training runs: $\lambda_{\text{VLM}}
\propto P \cdot \rho_{\text{fail}}$ where $P$ is run parallelism
and $\rho_{\text{fail}}$ is the per-step failure rate.  For
FetchPickAndPlace during the first $200{,}000$ steps,
$\rho_{\text{fail}} \approx 0.95$, so even a single run at
\texttt{vlm\_call\_interval}=1 makes $\sim 0.3$--$0.5$~calls/sec
wall-clock.  At $P=18$ this is $\sim 5$--$9$~calls/sec, an order
of magnitude above the Tier~1 RPM ceiling.

\paragraph{Mitigations for practitioners.}
Three strategies are effective in isolation; in practice a combination
is recommended:
\begin{enumerate}\itemsep0pt
  \item \textbf{Upgrade API tier.}  Anthropic Tier~2+ raises RPM
    to $\geq 50$~req/min, sufficient for $P \leq 6$ at
    \texttt{vlm\_call\_interval}=1. This is the simplest fix for
    large sweeps.
  \item \textbf{Dilute call rate via \texttt{cf\_call\_interval}.}
    Setting \texttt{cf\_call\_interval}$= k$ calls the VLM only on
    every $k$-th failed episode, reducing $\lambda_{\text{VLM}}$ by
    factor $k$.  For $k=8$ and $P=18$ the burst rate drops to
    $\sim 0.06$--$0.11$~calls/sec, well within Tier~1 limits.
    The trade-off is reduced counterfactual data density; empirically
    $k=4$--$8$ retains acceptable relabeling frequency.
  \item \textbf{Split VLM providers across runs.}  Routing half the
    seeds to GPT-4o (OpenAI) and half to a Claude model distributes
    the call load across independent rate-limit pools.  For our
    production pipeline, GPT-4o at \texttt{vlm\_call\_interval}=1
    remains within OpenAI Tier~1 limits up to $P \approx 12$.
\end{enumerate}
A batch-API approach (collecting failed episodes across a full epoch,
then submitting a single bulk request) is a fourth option for
offline or non-latency-critical replay augmentation, but it is
incompatible with the online episode-triggered call pattern used here.

\paragraph{Implications for reported experiments.}
Phase~2 attempts~3 and~4 of our in-flight Verified-CF sweep were
stopped after health checks revealed $73$\% none-rates.  Affected
runs are excluded from all quantitative comparisons.  The Phase~1
ablation (Section~\ref{sec:exp:headline}) uses \texttt{cf\_call\_interval}=8
throughout and was not affected; the Semantic-PER results reported there
use GPT-4o as provider and were collected before the claude-sonnet-4-5
switch, so no runs in Figure~\ref{fig:headline} are contaminated.

\section{Auxiliary Study: Replay-Mechanism Ablation on MetaWorld}
\label{app:metaworld}

This appendix supports the cross-benchmark corroboration discussed in
\S\ref{sec:disc:metaworld} and the methodological note on
demonstration-priority pinning in \S\ref{sec:related}
(``Demonstration-augmented replay''). The study is auxiliary to the
main paper: it does not run Semantic~PER or verified-counterfactual
hindsight, and is reported here only insofar as it (a)~independently
corroborates that the replay sampler is load-bearing in
sparse-reward continuous control, on a benchmark family
(MetaWorld~v3, \citep{yu2019metaworld}) that does not admit HER, and
(b)~documents the implementation-detail collapse between two
demo-augmented variants that motivates the methodological
recommendation in \S\ref{sec:related}.

\subsection{Setup}
\label{app:mw:setup}

\paragraph{Algorithm and tasks.}
The base agent is SAC~\citep{haarnoja2018sac} with twin critics,
automatic temperature tuning, two-layer 256-unit MLP actor and
critics, replay capacity $10^6$, batch size $256$, $\gamma=0.99$,
target-network rate $\tau=0.005$. Three MetaWorld~v3 manipulation
tasks were used: \texttt{drawer-open-v3}, \texttt{sweep-into-v3},
\texttt{button-press-v3}. The reward is sparse: the dense MetaWorld
shaping is discarded and the per-step reward is
$r_t = \texttt{float(info[``success''])}$, so all variants face the
same hard-exploration problem.

\paragraph{Replay-buffer variants.}
The four variants differ only in the buffer:
\begin{itemize}\itemsep0pt
  \item \textbf{uniform}: standard replay, every transition has
        equal sample probability.
  \item \textbf{per}: PER~\citep{schaul2016per} with $\alpha=0.6$,
        $\beta$ annealed $0.4\!\to\!1.0$ over training, priorities
        updated by $|\delta|$ after each gradient step.
  \item \textbf{demo-replay}: buffer pre-loaded with $K\!=\!10$
        expert episodes per task (5000 transitions) collected from
        MetaWorld's scripted policies (\texttt{ENV\_POLICY\_MAP}, pure
        expert, action noise $0.0$), sampled uniform thereafter.
  \item \textbf{demo-priority}: same pre-load, but every
        demonstration transition is inserted at a fixed priority of
        $10.0$ (vs.\ the $1.0$ default), with
        \texttt{update\_priorities} skipping demo indices so the
        priority floor never decays.
\end{itemize}
Comparing \textbf{demo-replay} against \textbf{uniform} isolates the
contribution of demonstration content; comparing
\textbf{demo-priority} against \textbf{demo-replay} was intended to
isolate the contribution of priority pinning at matched demonstration
content. \S\ref{app:mw:equiv} explains why the latter contrast turned
out to be empirically vacuous in our implementation.

\paragraph{Sweep.}
$3$ tasks $\times$ $4$ variants $\times$ $5$ seeds $= 60$ SAC runs at
$5{\times}10^5$ env steps each, on Modal L4 GPUs. Total compute:
$130.5$ L4-GPU-hours, $\$104.37$ at list rate. Evaluation every $10$k
env steps over $10$ episodes with greedy actions.

\subsection{The demo-replay $\equiv$ demo-priority implementation collapse}
\label{app:mw:equiv}

For every $(\text{task}, \text{seed})$ pair the two demo variants
produced \emph{bit-identical} eval curves
($0/15$ pairs differ; Figure~\ref{fig:mw:curves}). The cause is in
the buffer's insertion code
(\texttt{experiments/metaworld\_replay\_ablation/src/replay.py:71}):
{\small
\begin{verbatim}
p = priority if priority is not None else max(self._max_priority,
                                              self.default_priority)
\end{verbatim}}
\noindent which implements the Schaul~\citep{schaul2016per} ``insert
new transitions at the running $\max$-priority'' convention. Trace:

\begin{itemize}\itemsep0pt
  \item \textbf{demo-replay.} $5000$ demos are inserted with
        \texttt{priority=None}, so each receives
        $\max(1.0, 1.0) = 1.0$. All online transitions also receive
        $1.0$. The buffer's priorities are uniformly $1.0$; sampling
        reduces to uniform.
  \item \textbf{demo-priority.} $5000$ demos are inserted with
        $\texttt{priority}=10.0$, setting
        $\texttt{self.\_max\_priority}=10.0$. All subsequent online
        transitions enter at $\max(10.0, 1.0) = 10.0$. The buffer's
        priorities are uniformly $10.0$; sampling again reduces to
        uniform.
\end{itemize}

Because the sampling probabilities are
$p_i \!\propto\! p_i^\alpha / \sum_j p_j^\alpha$, a multiplicative
constant applied to every entry cancels in the denominator. The two
variants therefore execute identical sampling sequences under
identical random seeds, producing identical trajectories. This is
not a bug in the PER implementation---the Schaul convention is the
correct default for plain PER, where it ensures every transition is
seen at least once before its priority decays. It is an
\emph{interaction} between that convention and the demo-pinning
mechanism, and it erases the priority signal that the
\textbf{demo-priority} variant was meant to test.

A clean test of demonstration-priority pinning requires one of:
\begin{enumerate}\itemsep0pt
  \item Inserting online transitions at $\texttt{default\_priority}$
        (e.g., $1.0$) rather than at the running $\max$-priority, so
        demos retain their $10\times$ floor.
  \item Capping online priorities at a per-transition ceiling below
        the demo priority.
  \item Maintaining demo and online entries in two separate sample
        pools and mixing them with a fixed sampling
        ratio---the approach taken by
        \citet{nair2018overcomingexploration} and the DDPGfD/DAPG
        family~\citep{vecerik2017ddpgfd,rajeswaran2017dapg}.
\end{enumerate}
We pre-register option~3 as the cleanest follow-on test of the
demo-priority hypothesis, and Semantic~PER
(\S\ref{sec:method:sper}) as the principled alternative that does
not collapse under this convention: $w_{\text{sem}}$ varies
continuously across transitions, so the
constant-scale-invariance argument above does not apply.

\subsection{Limitations and pre-registered follow-ons}
\label{app:mw:limitations}

The study is small and auxiliary; we flag four limitations.
\textbf{(i)~Five seeds per cell} is below the $n\!\ge\!30$ standard
the main paper's headline numbers are pre-registered for. The
\texttt{sweep-into-v3} variance is high (some seeds saturate, others
fail completely); the CIs reflect this.
\textbf{(ii)~Three tasks only}: generalizing the
``demos saturate easy tasks but not hard ones'' pattern would
require a broader task set (MetaWorld MT10/MT50, or harder tasks
such as \texttt{pick-place} and \texttt{peg-insert-side}).
\textbf{(iii)~Pure scripted-expert demos}: noisy-demo ablations
(e.g.\ the DDPGfD setting where imperfect demos cover failure
recovery) were not run.
\textbf{(iv)~No Semantic-PER baseline}: a head-to-head comparison of
Semantic~PER against \texttt{uniform}, \texttt{per}, and the
demo-augmented variants on MetaWorld manipulation would directly
test whether the priority signal from a frozen VLM can solve the
harder task that demo content alone cannot (\texttt{sweep-into}),
and is pre-registered as the principal follow-on study.
