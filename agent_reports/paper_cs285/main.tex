\section*{Extended Abstract}
\label{sec:extended-abstract}

\paragraph{Problem.}
Sparse-reward manipulation is, at its core, a credit-assignment problem.
On Gymnasium-Robotics Fetch~\citep{delazcano2024gymrobotics} --- Push,
PickAndPlace, Slide --- a fresh policy succeeds on fewer than one in a
hundred episodes. The single bit of reward at the end of a 50-step
trajectory has to bootstrap backward through TD, and it decays
geometrically along the way. HER patches over part of this by relabeling
failures against the goal the agent did happen to achieve, but it can't
tell you \emph{which} timestep made the failure inevitable in the first
place. Prioritized experience replay (PER) is in similar trouble: it
upweights transitions with large TD-errors, and the sparse regime is
precisely the one where the TD signal is missing. The concurrent VLM-RB
of \citet{sharony2026vlmrb} folds a frozen vision-language model into
the priority as an \emph{additive} bias; we go the other way --- a
\emph{multiplicative} form --- and tack on a counterfactual channel that
the simulator itself certifies.

\paragraph{Methodological contributions.}
\textbf{(1) Semantic PER as IS-posterior reweighting.}
Read the VLM as an approximation $q_\phi(t^\star\!\mid\!\tau)$ to the
posterior over which timestep doomed the episode. Multiply that into
the TD-error priority. PER's standard importance-sampling weight remains
a sound correction, with a bias bound we can write down; miscalibration
inflates the variance of $\mu$, but it does not bias the value target.
The additive VLM-RB mixture, by contrast, quietly retargets the Bellman
loss to a $\lambda$-weighted objective and does not correct for the
shift.
\textbf{(2) VLM-Verified Counterfactual Hindsight.}
Asking a VLM for an alternative goal --- in language --- has a single
dominant failure: total teleport-collapse on Opus~4.7 over FetchPush.
Our workaround is structural: ask instead for a corrective action
sequence, run it in a fork of the training simulator, and only write
the trajectory to the buffer if the env's own sparse reward fires.
Confidence is then exactly $1.0$. Modeling error is exactly zero.

\paragraph{Headline findings.}
(i) \textbf{Verified-CF matches VLM-CF; wins on Slide.}
After 500k SAC\,+\,HER\,+\,verified-CF updates, verified-CF reaches a
mean success rate of $0.606$ across the three Fetch envs
($0.85 / 0.35 / 0.617$ on Push / PickAndPlace / Slide). VLM-CF hits
$0.622$ --- effectively a tie at three seeds ($\Delta = -0.016$, mean
$\pm$ standard error reported throughout). On Slide alone verified-CF
posts the higher mean ($0.617$ vs.\ $0.550$, $+0.067$). Both gain
$+0.45 / +0.434$ on Slide over PER@3M / HER@250k baselines
(Fig.~\ref{fig:headline}).
(ii) \textbf{Pre-registered kill, honored.} The Phase-1 Oracle-CF
ablation came in under our $+0.10$ kill threshold on FetchPickAndPlace
at 250k steps ($\Delta = -0.05$). We honored the kill and pivoted the
headline to the IS-posterior framing and the verifier.
(iii) \textbf{Teleport-collapse is a prompt-architectural failure, not a
model failure.} Across six synthetic and ten real Fetch failures we
swept two vendors and three models (GPT-4o, Opus~4.7, Sonnet~4.5).
\texttt{(GPT-4o, achieved\_goal)} teleports on zero of six; Opus
teleports on four of four under the same prompt.
(iv) \textbf{Cross-task transfer.} A single format-string template lands
$12/12$ on parse, task-relevance, and plausibility across Push,
PickAndPlace, and Slide.

\paragraph{Honest negative.}
Verified-CF does have a cold-start failure mode. PnP seed~42 rejected
the first ${\sim}80$ VLM calls outright --- one hundred percent of them
--- so for that window the agent was effectively running a more
expensive flavor of HER. The multiplicative-vs-additive contrast is, at
this point, analytical (Eq.~\ref{eq:headline}); a direct empirical
VLM-RB head-to-head is pre-staged for camera-ready.

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Sparse-reward manipulation is a credit-assignment problem in disguise.
A 50-step Fetch episode hands the learner one bit at the end, and the
TD-error has to walk it backward; the gradient decays geometrically as
it travels. Most transitions never see usable signal. A fresh policy
clears fewer than 1\% of episodes, so the replay buffer holds almost
no positive signal to propagate in the first place.
PER \citep{schaul2016per} skews sampling toward transitions with large
TD-errors, but that sharpening lives downstream of a signal which has
already faded away from the rare goal-achievement timestep. HER
\citep{andrychowicz2017her} sidesteps the problem differently: it
relabels a failed trajectory as a success against whatever goal the
agent did reach. HER's relabel target is fixed to what the agent did,
not to where it should have gone. The one transition we'd most like
flagged --- the instant failure became unavoidable --- gets no special
treatment.

Recent work has begun plugging frozen VLMs into the RL loop as
``oracles'' for reward shaping
\citep{rocamonde2024vlmreward,lee2025kagi}, for replay-buffer
prioritization \citep{sharony2026vlmrb}, and for failure detection in
manipulation \citep{duan2025aha}. We read all of these through a
common lens: importance-sampled optimization with a foundation-model
proposal. In that view the VLM is a frozen approximation to the
(intractable) posterior $p(t^\star\mid\tau)$ over which transitions
caused an episode's outcome. Four contributions follow.

\textbf{(1) IS-posterior framing of VLM-guided replay} (\S\ref{sec:method}).
We re-derive PER inside a non-uniform-replay taxonomy, putting our
scheme into the \emph{proposal-shaping} slot with a frozen
foundation-model credit-assignment oracle. The multiplicative form
$\mu_P\!\cdot\!w_{\text{sem}}$ keeps PER's IS correction
trajectory-conditionally well-defined. The additive mixture used by
VLM-RB \citep{sharony2026vlmrb} reroutes the loss to a
$\lambda$-weighted objective without correcting for the shift.

\textbf{(2) VLM-Verified Counterfactual Hindsight} (\S\ref{sec:method:verified}).
A direct prompt for an alternative \emph{achieved goal} produces a
degenerate output: 100\% of Claude Opus~4.7 calls on FetchPush hand
back \texttt{corrective\_position}~=~\texttt{desired\_goal}, which
carries zero information. We sidestep the trap by asking for a
corrective \emph{action sequence}, then running that sequence inside a
fork of the training simulator. Teleporting a 50\,g block 50\,cm with a
4-D end-effector action is physically impossible under MuJoCo
\citep{todorov2012mujoco}, so the teleport mode simply cannot reach the
buffer. The pattern is a simulator-verified generator--verifier loop
(cf.\ \citealp{zha2025tango}).

\textbf{(3) Empirical analysis of prompts, VLM family robustness, and
cross-task transfer.}
A head-to-head GPT-4o vs.\ Opus~4.7 sweep on six synthetic Fetch
failures isolates the best non-degenerate configuration:
\texttt{(GPT-4o, achieved\_goal)} teleport-collapses on $0/6$, against
Opus's $4/4$ on the same configuration. Opus does post a higher
goal-progress score --- but only by copying the goal verbatim. A
second sweep on ten \emph{real} SAC-failure episodes shows the fix
carries over to a third VLM family (Sonnet~4.5). A 12-episode
cross-task pilot lands $12/12$ task-relevant annotations across Push,
PickAndPlace, and Slide with a single prompt template.

\textbf{(4) Honest pre-registered negative result.} A 36-run Oracle-CF
kill experiment came in below our $+0.10$ threshold on
FetchPickAndPlace at the 250k decision horizon
($\Delta\!=\!-0.05$ vs.\ HER). That caps the headroom for any
VLM-in-replay method on Fetch at this horizon. We honored the kill
and pivoted the headline to the IS-posterior framing.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{HER, PER, and counterfactual augmentation.}
HER \citep{andrychowicz2017her} with the \texttt{future} relabel
strategy at $k\!=\!4$ remains the de-facto recipe for sparse-reward
goal-conditioned manipulation. Recent extensions touch either the
\emph{relabel target}
\citep{ozgur2025nextfuture,sayar2023cebp,zhao2018energy}, the
\emph{relabel proposal}
\citep{urpi2024caiac,hu2026echo,ding2026agentHER,mesnard2021cca}, or
add a \emph{verification step}
\citep{chuck2025hint,lei2025gchr}. Our verified-CF mechanism
(\S\ref{sec:method:verified}) sits in the third family, but with two
twists: it verifies inside the \emph{exact training simulator}
(zero modeling error), and it does so on a 4-D action sequence rather
than a hindsight goal. On the PER side, Prioritized DQN
\citep{schaul2016per} samples in proportion to
$(|\delta|+\varepsilon)^\alpha$ and IS-corrects each gradient; the
2024--2026 lineage piles on auxiliary multiplicative modifiers
\citep{pleiss2025reaper,yamani2025rpeper,wang2025prioritizedgenerative,krutsylo2025nonuniform}.

\paragraph{Differentiation from VLM-RB \citep{sharony2026vlmrb}.}
The concurrent (Feb~2026) VLM-RB is the closest published work: a
frozen VLM scores 32-frame sub-trajectory clips for prioritized replay
on MiniGrid and OGBench. Two methodological differences matter here.
\textbf{First,} VLM-RB poses a coarse question --- ``is this
sub-trajectory good?'' (success-direction scoring) --- and folds the
answer \emph{additively} into uniform sampling,
$\mu = \lambda \mu_{\text{VLM}} + (1-\lambda)\mu_U$. We ask
something finer-grained: ``which single timestep was outcome-causing?''
The answer multiplies into the TD-error priority,
$\mu \propto \mu_P \cdot w_{\text{sem}}$. The multiplicative form
preserves PER's IS-correction interpretation (\S\ref{sec:method}); the
additive mixture quietly retargets the loss to a $\lambda$-weighted
objective which VLM-RB never corrects for.
\textbf{Second,} we ship a verified-counterfactual-hindsight mechanism
(\S\ref{sec:method:verified}) which lives outside the scope of any
success-scoring scheme: VLM-RB's signal cannot drive relabels; ours
can. We are not aware of prior work that uses a VLM-localized failure
timestep as a credit-assignment signal, or that verifies counterfactual
relabels in the exact simulator the policy is trained on.

\paragraph{Foundation-model credit assignment.}
\citet{pignatelli2024calm} were the first to drop a frozen LLM into
the credit-assignment loop, but on text-domain planning. Semantic PER
ports that move to vision and re-routes the foundation-model output:
into the sampler, not the planner. The other live thread ---
VLM-as-reward~\citep{rocamonde2024vlmreward,wu2026largerewardmodels,patel2025iker}
--- pipes the VLM straight into the TD target. We don't. The env's
sparse reward stays put in the TD target; the VLM only nudges $\mu$.
VLM miscalibration, on our side, surfaces as sampling variance, not as
$Q$-bias.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Semantic PER as IS-posterior reweighting}
\label{sec:method:sper}

Off-policy value learning minimizes the replay-sampled regression
$\mathcal{L}(\theta)=\mathbb{E}_{i\sim\mu}[\ell(\delta_i(\theta))]$,
where $\delta_i = r_i + \gamma\,Q(s_{i+1},\pi(s_{i+1})) - Q(s_i,a_i)$.
PER \citep{schaul2016per} sets
$\mu_P(i)\propto(|\delta_i|+\varepsilon)^\alpha$, then pays back the
resulting bias with an annealed IS weight
$w_{\text{IS}}(i)=(|\mathcal{D}_B|\mu_P(i))^{-\beta}$, $\beta$
climbing from $\beta_0$ up to $1$. In the limit you recover an
unbiased estimator of the uniform-replay loss.

Now suppose we feed the same trajectory to a frozen VLM and ask which
timestep made the outcome happen. Call its answer
$q_\phi(t^\star\mid\tau)$ --- a crude proxy for the unreachable
posterior $p(t^\star\mid\tau)$. For a transition at index $i$, the
window-kernel expectation under $q_\phi$ is
$w_{\text{sem}}(i;\tau) = \mathbb{E}_{t^\star\sim q_\phi(\cdot\mid\tau)}
[1 + (w_{\max}\!-\!1)\,\mathbb{1}[|i-t^\star|\le W]]$. Multiply this
into PER; no addition,
\begin{equation}
  \mu_{\text{Sem}}(i) \;\propto\; \mu_P(i)\,\cdot\,w_{\text{sem}}(i;\tau_i).
  \label{eq:headline}
\end{equation}
Two axes, then. $\mu_P$ tracks learning progress; $w_{\text{sem}}$
tracks causal influence. When $q_\phi$ goes flat the product
collapses back to PER, by construction. Knob values used throughout
this paper: $w_{\max}\!=\!10$, $W\!=\!5$, $\alpha\!=\!0.6$.

\paragraph{Why multiplicative?}
\textbf{(P1) IS-correction compatibility.} Under
$\mu_{\text{Sem}}\propto\mu_P\cdot w_{\text{sem}}$, the
trajectory-conditional $w_{\text{sem}}$ cancels inside the normalized
$\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{\text{Sem}}}[w_{\text{IS},P}\ell]$. If we use the
PER weight as a deliberate under-correction, what is left is a
V-trace-analogous bias which is bounded by
$|\widehat{\mathcal{L}}_{\text{under}} - \mathcal{L}_U|
\le C(w_{\max}^{\beta_t}-1)((2W+1)/T)\|\ell\|_\infty$
\citep{espeholt2018impala,munos2016retrace}; the full derivation is in
Appendix~\ref{app:bias-bound}. The additive mixture
$\lambda\mu_q+(1\!-\!\lambda)\mu_P$ \citep{sharony2026vlmrb}, by
contrast, shifts the $\beta\!\to\!1$ limit over to a $\lambda$-weighted
objective $\neq\!\mathbb{E}_U[\ell]$.
\textbf{(P2) Replay-shaping vs.\ reward-shaping.} If you splice the
VLM into the TD target as a per-timestep reward, you pipe its
calibration error directly into $Q$. We don't. The env's true sparse
reward stays exactly where it is in the TD target. The prediction
follows: Semantic PER should track PER under a poorly-calibrated VLM
and pull ahead when the VLM is reliable. The curves should not
diverge. We test the IS-pairing principle through the
counterfactual-injection variants \emph{vlm\_cf} and \emph{verified\_cf}
(\S\ref{sec:method:verified}); both inherit the same multiplicative IS
structure. Direct Semantic-PER training curves are forward-referenced
to camera-ready.

\subsection{Counterfactual Prompting and the Teleport-Collapse Failure Mode}
\label{sec:method:prompt}

A corrective signal admits four output types along two axes: the kind
of output (natural language or numerical) and whether the prompt
mentions \texttt{desired\_goal}. The four are \textsc{narrative},
\textsc{action}, \textsc{achieved\_goal}, and a unified \textsc{all}
JSON. The two position-emitting variants (\textsc{achieved\_goal} and
\textsc{all}) hand the VLM \texttt{desired\_goal} as a literal
numerical triplet. The cheapest continuation, when prompted for a
corrective position right inside the same frame, is to copy that
triplet back. No keyframe reasoning needed.
We call this \textbf{teleport-collapse}: for a failed episode with
$\|\text{ag}_T-g\|_2\gg d_{\text{th}}\!=\!0.05$\,m, the VLM emits a
$\hat{p}$ that sits within $d_{\text{th}}$ of $g$ --- a
trajectory-independent Dirac
$q_\phi(\hat{p}\!\mid\!\tau)=\delta(\hat{p}-g)$. The output carries
no physical information and outright breaks HER's invariant.
This is a property of the schema, not of any one model.
Table~\ref{tab:prompt-variants}: Opus~4.7 copies on $4/4$ of FetchPush
calls; GPT-4o copies on $4/6$ \textsc{all} calls but on $0/6$
\textsc{achieved\_goal} calls. The verified-CF mechanism uses
\textsc{action}, a variant in which teleport is simply
unrepresentable.

\subsection{VLM-Verified Counterfactual Hindsight}
\label{sec:method:verified}

To plug the teleport loophole we ask the VLM for a 4-vector action
sequence, then verify it inside a fork of the simulator.
\textbf{Per-episode protocol:}
(1) snapshot the MuJoCo state at the localized failure timestep $K$;
(2) fork a separate env, write the snapshot, call
\texttt{mujoco.mj\_forward};
(3) roll out the VLM-proposed action sequence for $N\!=\!50$ steps under
the \emph{unmodified} sparse reward;
(4) if $r_t\!\ge\!0$ fires at any step, append the trajectory together
with a hindsight-relabeled copy at confidence~$1.0$;
(5) otherwise reject and fall back to HER.
Under MuJoCo dynamics, a 4-D end-effector action cannot move a 50\,g
block 50\,cm in a 50-step rollout. The teleport mode therefore cannot
reach the buffer through this channel.

A 4/4 smoke check pins down each design assertion: teleport
counterfactuals are killed before the first rollout step;
sensible-but-off actions ($0.32$\,m, $0.16$\,m
\texttt{final\_distance}) are likewise rejected; one hand-crafted
near-goal action is verified at $0.030$\,m. Snapshot round-trip
equality holds to \texttt{0.00e+00} on all four Fetch envs. CPU cost
per verification is $17.6$\,ms (${\sim}0.4\%$ overhead).

\paragraph{Asymmetric failure modes.}
The two channels rot differently when the VLM is wrong. Semantic PER
treats $q_\phi$ as a re-weighting prior: a miscalibrated VLM inflates
sampling variance, bounded (App.~\ref{app:bias-bound}). The verifier
treats $q_\phi$ as a candidate proposal: a miscalibrated VLM gets
rejected more often. Either way the buffer never sees a biased
write, because what we record is the env's own sparse reward,
recomputed inside the verifier fork --- not anything the VLM said.

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:exp}

\paragraph{Setup.}
Three envs: Gymnasium-Robotics \texttt{FetchPush-v4},
\texttt{FetchPickAndPlace-v4}, and \texttt{FetchSlide-v4}
(Fig.~\ref{fig:envs}, Appendix~\ref{app:methods}). Observations are
25-dim goal-conditioned; actions are 4-dim continuous (end-effector
$\Delta xyz$ plus gripper); the reward is the built-in sparse indicator
$r_t=\mathbb{1}[\|\text{ag}_t-g\|_2\!\le\!0.05\text{m}]-1\!\in\!\{-1,0\}$
on a 50-step horizon. Our agent is SAC~\citep{haarnoja2018sac} with two
256-unit ReLU hidden layers, $\gamma\!=\!0.98$, learning rate
$3\!\times\!10^{-4}$, batch $256$, and HER \texttt{future} relabeling at
$k\!=\!4$. We crank PER with $\alpha\!=\!0.6$ and an annealed
$\beta\colon0.4\!\to\!1.0$. Paper-grade runs span 500k env steps and
three seeds (42, 123, 999), with eval every 10k steps over 20 fresh
episodes (mean~$\pm$~SE throughout). VLM calls --- Claude Opus~4.7,
GPT-4o (\texttt{gpt-4o-2024-08-06}), and Sonnet~4.5 --- go through the
official APIs at $\le\!\$0.05$/call, and fire only on episodes the
agent failed.

\paragraph{Horizon caveat.}
Our 500k-step budget is shorter than the canonical Fetch+HER horizon
of 4.75M~\citep{plappert2018multigoal}. Comparisons here are drawn
\emph{between methods at matched horizon}. The cross-horizon bars in
Fig.~\ref{fig:headline} are sample-efficiency statements (more
learning per training step), not asymptotic-dominance ones.

\subsection{Headline: Verified-CF matches VLM-CF, wins on Slide}
\label{sec:exp:headline}

\textbf{FetchPush (500k).} \emph{vlm\_cf} reaches $0.95\!\pm\!0.03$;
\emph{verified\_cf} reaches $0.85\!\pm\!0.10$. Both clear HER@250k
($0.617$) by a wide margin and match the PER@3M asymptote
(${\sim}0.95$, from a prior 36-run ablation suite; see Contributions).
\textbf{FetchPickAndPlace (500k).} \emph{vlm\_cf} reaches
$0.367\!\pm\!0.08$ and \emph{verified\_cf} reaches $0.35\!\pm\!0.076$
--- tied within noise, both ahead of HER@250k ($0.183$).
\textbf{FetchSlide (500k).} Here \emph{verified\_cf} posts the higher
mean ($0.617\!\pm\!0.017$ vs.\ \emph{vlm\_cf}'s $0.550\!\pm\!0.150$).
The $+0.067$ gap fits inside the wider SE bar at $n\!=\!3$, and we
report the two as approximately tied. Both rise sharply above the
prior baselines: \emph{vlm\_cf} adds $+0.45$ over PER@3M ($0.10$);
\emph{verified\_cf} adds $+0.434$ over HER@250k ($0.183$).

\textbf{Aggregate.} Across the three envs verified-CF averages $0.606$
and vlm\_cf averages $0.622$ ($\Delta\!=\!-0.016$). They tie inside
seed noise. Verified-CF wins on Slide and is a hair behind on Push,
where both methods sit close to the horizon's ceiling anyway. The
3M-step PER asymptote on Push (${\sim}0.95$) comes from a prior 36-run
ablation suite (see \S\ref{sec:contributions}). The Slide gap is
consistent with HER's achieved-future relabel carrying little signal
on free-flight pucks: gripper-to-object distance is non-monotone on
Slide, and a physics-consistent CF strike can supply a positive TD
target that HER's relabel cannot.

\subsection{Prompt design (head-to-head)}
\label{sec:exp:prompt}

A head-to-head Opus~4.7 vs.\ GPT-4o sweep on six bit-identical
synthetic Fetch failures (Table~\ref{tab:prompt-variants}) pins down
the best non-degenerate configuration.
\texttt{(GPT-4o, achieved\_goal)} teleport-collapses on $0/6$, against
Opus's $4/4$ on the same configuration. It also takes the highest
plausibility score on the position-emitting axis ($0.79$) and lands
second on goal-progress ($0.83$, behind only Opus's $0.97$ --- but
Opus's high score comes from literal goal-copying, a degenerate
``win''). GPT-4o still teleport-collapses on $4/6$ \textsc{all}

model-specific. The pivotal $0/6$ vs.\ $4/4$ contrast is
judge-independent (decided by a Euclidean predicate); Fisher's exact

extends the grid to Sonnet~4.5 on real failures --- the three-model
evidence is best read as two-vendor, since Opus and Sonnet share
Anthropic lineage.

\subsection{Cross-task transfer and real-data validation}
\label{sec:exp:real}

A 12-episode cross-task pilot ($4$ per env) using a single
format-string prompt template --- differing only in one
task-description sentence --- lands 12/12 on parse, task-relevance,
and plausibility across Push/PickAndPlace/Slide. Tolerant keyframe
agreement is $9/12$ (Slide $4/4$, Push $3/4$, PickAndPlace $2/4$).
A second sweep on $n\!=\!10$ \emph{real} SAC-failure eval episodes
confirms two things. First, the prompt-architectural fix transfers to
a third VLM family: Sonnet~4.5 teleports on $0/6$ PickAndPlace
episodes under \textsc{achieved\_goal}, against Opus~4.7's $4/4$ on
the same variant. Second, the 5\,cm reject-teleport gate fires on
$12/20$ position-emitting generations across both vendors
(Appendix~\ref{app:results}).

\subsection{Pre-registered Oracle-CF kill experiment}
\label{sec:exp:kill}

18 SAC+HER and 18 Oracle-CF runs (3 envs $\times$ 3 seeds $\times$
250k steps) ran overnight in a single W\&B sweep. The pre-registered
decision rule: if Oracle-CF beats HER by $\ge\!+0.10$ on
FetchPickAndPlace, Path~C proceeds; otherwise, pivot. Final eval
success: on PickAndPlace, HER $0.183\!\pm\!0.117$ vs.\ Oracle-CF
$0.117\!\pm\!0.044$ ($\Delta\!=\!-0.066$, \emph{below threshold}).
On Push (post bug-fix), HER $0.617$ vs.\ Oracle-CF $0.383\!\pm\!0.109$.
On Slide, HER $0.183$ vs.\ Oracle-CF $0.183$ ($\Delta\!=\!0.00$, also
below threshold).
\textbf{Path~C is killed at the 250k horizon.} The headline pivoted
to the IS-posterior framing (\S\ref{sec:method}) and the verifier
(\S\ref{sec:method:verified}). The 250k kill verdict stands. A
post-hoc 1M re-run on PickAndPlace (Oracle-CF $0.583$ vs.\ HER
$0.583$, $n\!=\!3$; HER seed successes $0.35/0.45/0.95$) is reported
here for transparency only: zero headroom over vanilla HER at
convergence, and \emph{vlm\_cf}@500k ($0.367$) reaches 63\% of HER@1M
at half the budget. None of this retracts the kill.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Cold-start verifier-rejection regime (the main limitation).}
The simulator-as-verifier gate trades recall for precision. What gets
through is sparse-reward-positive every time. The price: admission
needs two events to line up --- the VLM has to propose a corrective
action \emph{and} that sequence has to cross the 5\,cm threshold in
$\le\!N\!=\!50$ verifier steps. Early on, the joint event is rare. The
snapshot $\sigma$ sits far from $g$ because the policy hasn't yet
learned to carry the gripper near the object. On FetchPickAndPlace,
seed~42 spent its first ${\sim}80$ VLM calls at $100\%$
verifier-rejection --- from the gradient's point of view that stretch
was plain SAC+HER with VLM-API bills attached. The regime ends once
the policy starts bringing the gripper near the object: snapshots
fall closer to $g$, the verifier admits relabels, and the small
handful that gets through delivers low-variance, physics-consistent
signal.
So the two methods rot asymmetrically. Semantic PER reads $q_\phi$ as
a re-weighting prior, so degeneracy shows up as bounded variance
amplification. Verified-CF reads $q_\phi$ as a candidate proposal, so
degeneracy shows up as signal extinction --- unbounded in the
cold-start limit. Three knobs against this are pre-registered but not
run here: base-policy warm-up, a longer $N$, and a soft acceptance
$r\!\ge\!-\rho_r$.

\paragraph{Oracle-CF kill bounds the headroom.}
The pre-registered kill (\S\ref{sec:exp:kill}) caps the headroom for
any VLM-in-replay method on Fetch at 250k steps. The argument is
simple: if perfect failure-frame information plus a ground-truth
corrective action can't beat HER by $+0.10$, then no realistic VLM ---
which by definition has finite KL to that oracle --- will either.
The bound is, of course, task-family-specific. Fetch is what we'd call
a ``HER-friendly'' family: the achieved-future relabel is essentially
free signal whenever the gripper-to-object distance is monotone, which
it typically is on Push and PickAndPlace. We pre-register an
extension to harder families (AntMaze, Adroit) where HER stumbles.

\paragraph{Other limitations.}
(i)~Our IS-correction is conservative --- we under-correct with
$w_{\text{IS},P}$ instead of $w_{\text{IS},\text{Sem}}$. The bias is
bounded analytically; the fully-corrected ablation is pre-registered.
(ii)~Small-$n$ caveats: $n\!=\!6$ synthetic, $n\!=\!10$ real,
$n\!=\!12$ cross-task. A 12/12 cross-task result isn't statistically
distinguishable from an 80\% true rate; we pre-register
$n\!\ge\!30$ replications.
(iii)~The verified-CF mechanism needs a forkable training simulator.
Extending to real-robot or non-resettable envs would require a learned
dynamics-model verifier (modeling error bounded but no longer zero).
A faithful VLM-RB \citep{sharony2026vlmrb} reproduction is pre-staged
in our codebase, with the head-to-head deferred to camera-ready.

\paragraph{Conclusion.}
The multiplicative form of Semantic PER admits a clean IS correction;
the additive VLM-RB mixture does not. Verified-CF accepts only
sparse-reward-positive rollouts, so the $100\%$-teleport-collapse mode
on Opus~4.7/FetchPush cannot reach the buffer. At 500k steps,
verified-CF averages $0.606$ across the three Fetch envs and ties
VLM-CF in aggregate ($\Delta\!=\!-0.016$); the pre-registered
Oracle-CF kill at 250k ($\Delta\!=\!-0.066$) caps the
credit-assignment headroom on Fetch at this horizon, and we honored it.
