\section*{Extended Abstract}
\label{sec:extended-abstract}

\paragraph{Problem.}
Sparse-reward manipulation is a credit-assignment problem. On
Gymnasium-Robotics Fetch~\citep{delazcano2024gymrobotics} (Push /
PickAndPlace / Slide) a fresh policy succeeds on under $1\%$ of episodes,
and the single terminal reward bit at the end of a 50-step trajectory
decays geometrically as it is bootstrapped backward through TD. HER
relabels failed trajectories with realized achieved goals, but it never
says \emph{which} timestep made failure inevitable. Prioritized
experience replay (PER) is also stuck: it sharpens toward high-TD-error
transitions, and in the sparse regime the TD signal is exactly what is
missing. Concurrent VLM-RB \citep{sharony2026vlmrb} adds a frozen
vision-language model (VLM) score as an \emph{additive} priority bias;
we take the \emph{multiplicative} form instead, and bolt on a
simulator-verified counterfactual channel.

\paragraph{Methodological contributions.}
\textbf{(1) Semantic PER as IS-posterior reweighting.}
Treat the VLM as an approximation $q_\phi(t^\star\!\mid\!\tau)$ to the
posterior over the failure-causing timestep. Multiply the TD-error priority
by $q_\phi$. The standard PER weight remains a sound IS correction with
a derivable bias bound; miscalibration shows up as variance inflation in
$\mu$, never as bias in the value target. The additive VLM-RB mixture
silently retargets the Bellman loss to a $\lambda$-weighted objective it
does not correct.
\textbf{(2) VLM-Verified Counterfactual Hindsight.}
Language-only counterfactuals have a dominant failure mode: 100\%
teleport-collapse on Opus~4.7 / FetchPush. Our fix asks the VLM for a
\emph{corrective action sequence}, executes it in a fork of the training
simulator, and writes the trajectory to the buffer only if the env's own
sparse reward fires. Confidence is then 1.0 and the modeling error is
zero.

\paragraph{Headline findings.}
(i) \textbf{Verified-CF matches VLM-CF; wins on Slide.}
After 500k SAC\,+\,HER\,+\,verified-CF updates verified-CF reaches mean
success $0.606$ across the three Fetch envs ($0.85 / 0.35 / 0.617$ on
Push / PickAndPlace / Slide). VLM-CF reaches $0.622$ — a tie within seed
noise ($\Delta = -0.016$, $n\!=\!3$, $\pm$ standard error throughout).
On Slide alone, verified-CF leads ($0.617$ vs.\ $0.550$, $+0.067$).
Both gain $+0.45 / +0.434$ on Slide over PER@3M / HER@250k baselines
(Fig.~\ref{fig:headline}).
(ii) \textbf{Pre-registered kill: honored.} The Phase-1 Oracle-CF
ablation violated our $+0.10$ kill threshold on FetchPickAndPlace at the
250k decision horizon ($\Delta = -0.05$). We honored it and pivoted the
headline to the IS-posterior framing and the verifier.
(iii) \textbf{Teleport-collapse is a prompt-architectural failure, not a
model failure.} On six synthetic and ten real Fetch failures we swept
two vendors and three models (GPT-4o / Opus~4.7 / Sonnet~4.5):
\texttt{(GPT-4o, achieved\_goal)} teleports on $0/6$, Opus teleports on
$4/4$ of the same configuration.
(iv) \textbf{Cross-task transfer.} A single format-string prompt template
yields $12/12$ parse / task-relevance / plausibility on Push /
PickAndPlace / Slide.

\paragraph{Honest negative.}
Verified-CF has a cold-start failure mode. PnP seed~42 rejected the first
$\sim\!80$ VLM calls at $100\%$, so the agent ran as a more expensive HER
for that window. The multiplicative-vs-additive distinction is analytical
(Eq.~\ref{eq:headline}); a direct VLM-RB head-to-head is pre-staged for
camera-ready.

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Sparse-reward manipulation is a credit-assignment problem.
A 50-step episode emits one reward bit at the terminal timestep, and
TD-error decays geometrically as the signal bootstraps backward through
the trajectory. Most transitions never see a usable gradient. On
Gymnasium-Robotics Fetch the early policy succeeds on under 1\% of
episodes, so the replay buffer holds almost no positive signal to
propagate backward in the first place.
PER \citep{schaul2016per} sharpens sampling toward high-TD-error
transitions, but the sharpening sits downstream of a credit signal that
itself decays away from the rare goal-achievement timestep. Hindsight
Experience Replay \citep{andrychowicz2017her} fixes part of this by
relabeling failures as successes against an achieved goal, but the
relabel target is constrained to what the agent actually did. The one
transition we most want flagged — the moment failure became inevitable
— receives no special treatment.

Recent work plugs frozen VLMs into the RL loop as ``oracles'' for
reward shaping \citep{rocamonde2024vlmreward,lee2025kagi},
replay-buffer prioritization \citep{sharony2026vlmrb}, and failure
detection in manipulation \citep{duan2025aha}. We read all of these as
importance-sampled stochastic optimization with a foundation-model
proposal distribution: the VLM is a frozen approximation to the
(intractable) posterior $p(t^\star\mid\tau)$ over which transitions
caused an episode's outcome. Four contributions follow.

\textbf{(1) IS-posterior framing of VLM-guided replay} (\S\ref{sec:method}).
We re-derive PER inside a non-uniform-replay taxonomy, placing our scheme
as \emph{proposal-shaping} with a frozen foundation-model credit-assignment
oracle. The multiplicative form $\mu_P\!\cdot\!w_{\text{sem}}$ keeps PER's
IS correction trajectory-conditionally well-defined. The additive mixture
used by VLM-RB \citep{sharony2026vlmrb} retargets the loss to a
$\lambda$-weighted objective and does not correct for the shift.

\textbf{(2) VLM-Verified Counterfactual Hindsight} (\S\ref{sec:method:verified}).
A direct prompt for an alternative \emph{achieved goal} fails: 100\% of
Claude Opus~4.7 calls on FetchPush return
\texttt{corrective\_position}~=~\texttt{desired\_goal}, a degenerate
zero-information output. Our fix is to ask for a corrective
\emph{action sequence} instead, then execute it in a fork of the training
simulator. Teleporting a $50$\,g block by $50$\,cm with a 4-D
end-effector action is physically impossible in MuJoCo
\citep{todorov2012mujoco}, so the teleport mode cannot reach the
buffer. This is a simulator-verified generator--verifier protocol
(cf.\ \citealp{zha2025tango}).

\textbf{(3) Empirical analysis of prompts, VLM family robustness, and
cross-task transfer.}
A head-to-head GPT-4o vs.\ Claude Opus~4.7 sweep on six synthetic Fetch
failures pins down a best non-degenerate configuration:
\texttt{(GPT-4o, achieved\_goal)} teleport-collapses on 0/6, against
Opus's 4/4 of the same configuration. Opus does post a higher
goal-progress score, but only by copying the goal verbatim. A second
sweep on ten \emph{real} SAC-failure episodes shows the fix carries over
to a third VLM family (Sonnet~4.5). A 12-episode cross-task pilot lands
12/12 task-relevant annotations on Push / PickAndPlace / Slide with a
single prompt template.

\textbf{(4) Honest pre-registered negative result.} A 36-run Oracle-CF
kill experiment came in under our $+0.10$ threshold on FetchPickAndPlace
at the 250k decision horizon ($\Delta\!=\!-0.05$ vs.\ HER). This caps the
headroom for any VLM-in-replay method on Fetch at that horizon. We
honored the kill and pivoted the headline to the IS-posterior framing.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{HER, PER, and counterfactual augmentation.}
Hindsight Experience Replay \citep{andrychowicz2017her} with the
\texttt{future} relabel strategy and $k\!=\!4$ remains the dominant
solution to sparse-reward goal-conditioned manipulation. Recent
extensions touch the \emph{relabel target}
\citep{ozgur2025nextfuture,sayar2023cebp,zhao2018energy}, the
\emph{relabel proposal} \citep{urpi2024caiac,hu2026echo,ding2026agentHER,mesnard2021cca},
or add a \emph{verification step} \citep{chuck2025hint,lei2025gchr};
our verified-CF mechanism (\S\ref{sec:method:verified}) is in the
third family but verifies in the \emph{exact training simulator}
(zero modeling error) on an \emph{action sequence} rather than a
hindsight goal. Prioritized DQN \citep{schaul2016per} samples in
proportion to $(|\delta|+\varepsilon)^\alpha$ and IS-corrects each
gradient; the 2024--2026 PER lineage explores auxiliary
multiplicative modifiers \citep{pleiss2025reaper,yamani2025rpeper,wang2025prioritizedgenerative,krutsylo2025nonuniform}.

\paragraph{Differentiation from VLM-RB \citep{sharony2026vlmrb}.}
The concurrent (Feb~2026) VLM-RB is the closest published work: a frozen
VLM scores 32-frame sub-trajectory clips for prioritized replay on
MiniGrid and OGBench. Two methodological differences matter here.
\textbf{First,} VLM-RB asks ``is this sub-trajectory good?''
(success-direction scoring) and mixes the answer \emph{additively} with
uniform sampling, $\mu = \lambda \mu_{\text{VLM}} + (1-\lambda)\mu_U$.
We ask a finer-grained question — ``which single timestep was
outcome-causing?'' — and combine it \emph{multiplicatively} with the
TD-error priority, $\mu \propto \mu_P \cdot w_{\text{sem}}$. The
multiplicative form preserves PER's IS-correction interpretation
(\S\ref{sec:method}); the additive mixture silently retargets the loss
to a $\lambda$-weighted objective that VLM-RB does not correct.
\textbf{Second,} we ship a verified-counterfactual-hindsight mechanism
(\S\ref{sec:method:verified}) that lives outside the scope of any
success-scoring scheme: VLM-RB's signal cannot drive relabels; ours can.
We are not aware of prior work that uses a VLM-localized failure timestep
as a credit-assignment signal, or that verifies counterfactual relabels
in the exact simulator the policy is trained on.

\paragraph{Foundation-model credit assignment.}
\citet{pignatelli2024calm} first dropped a frozen LLM into the
credit-assignment loop, but on text-domain planning. Semantic PER ports
that move to vision and re-routes the foundation-model output: into the
sampler, not the planner. The other live thread —
VLM-as-reward~\citep{rocamonde2024vlmreward,wu2026largerewardmodels,patel2025iker}
— pipes the VLM into the TD target. We don't touch the TD target. The
env's sparse reward stays untouched there; the VLM only nudges $\mu$.
VLM miscalibration shows up as sampling variance, never as $Q$-bias.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Semantic PER as IS-posterior reweighting}
\label{sec:method:sper}

Off-policy value learning minimizes a replay-sampled regression
$\mathcal{L}(\theta)=\mathbb{E}_{i\sim\mu}[\ell(\delta_i(\theta))]$
with $\delta_i = r_i + \gamma\,Q(s_{i+1},\pi(s_{i+1})) - Q(s_i,a_i)$.
PER \citep{schaul2016per} sets
$\mu_P(i)\propto(|\delta_i|+\varepsilon)^\alpha$ and pays back the bias
with an annealed IS weight,
$w_{\text{IS}}(i)=(|\mathcal{D}_B|\mu_P(i))^{-\beta}$, $\beta$ climbing
from $\beta_0$ up to $1$. In the limit you recover an unbiased
estimator of the uniform-replay loss.

Now suppose we hand the same trajectory to a frozen VLM and ask which
timestep made the outcome happen. Call the VLM's answer
$q_\phi(t^\star\mid\tau)$ — a crude stand-in for the unreachable
posterior $p(t^\star\mid\tau)$. For a transition at index $i$, the
window-kernel expectation under $q_\phi$ is
$w_{\text{sem}}(i;\tau) = \mathbb{E}_{t^\star\sim q_\phi(\cdot\mid\tau)}
[1 + (w_{\max}\!-\!1)\,\mathbb{1}[|i-t^\star|\le W]]$.
Multiply that into PER, no addition,
\begin{equation}
  \mu_{\text{Sem}}(i) \;\propto\; \mu_P(i)\,\cdot\,w_{\text{sem}}(i;\tau_i).
  \label{eq:headline}
\end{equation}
$\mu_P$ holds the learning-progress axis; $w_{\text{sem}}$ holds the
causal-influence axis. When $q_\phi$ goes flat the product collapses
back to PER. Knob values throughout this paper: $w_{\max}\!=\!10$,
$W\!=\!5$, $\alpha\!=\!0.6$.

\paragraph{Why multiplicative?}
\textbf{(P1) IS-correction compatibility.} Under
$\mu_{\text{Sem}}\propto\mu_P\cdot w_{\text{sem}}$, the
trajectory-conditional $w_{\text{sem}}$ factor cancels in the normalized
$\mathbb{E}_{\mu_{\text{Sem}}}[w_{\text{IS},P}\ell]$. Using the PER
weight as an under-correction then leaves a V-trace-analogous bias that
is bounded by
$|\widehat{\mathcal{L}}_{\text{under}} - \mathcal{L}_U|
\le C(w_{\max}^{\beta_t}-1)((2W+1)/T)\|\ell\|_\infty$
\citep{espeholt2018impala,munos2016retrace}; the full derivation lives
in \texttt{src/buffers/semantic\_per.py} on GitHub and in
Appendix~\ref{app:methods}. The additive mixture
$\lambda\mu_q+(1\!-\!\lambda)\mu_P$ \citep{sharony2026vlmrb} shifts the
$\beta\!\to\!1$ limit to a $\lambda$-weighted objective
$\neq\!\mathbb{E}_U[\ell]$.
\textbf{(P2) Replay-shaping vs.\ reward-shaping.} Splicing the VLM into
the TD target as a per-timestep reward feeds VLM calibration error
straight into $Q$. We do not. The env's true sparse reward stays
intact in the TD target. Prediction: Semantic PER should track PER
under a poorly-calibrated VLM and pull ahead when the VLM is reliable;
the curves should not diverge. We test the IS-pairing principle
through the counterfactual-injection variants \emph{vlm\_cf} and
\emph{verified\_cf} (\S\ref{sec:method:verified}), which inherit the
same multiplicative IS structure. Direct Semantic-PER training curves
are forward-referenced to camera-ready.

\subsection{Counterfactual Prompting and the Teleport-Collapse Failure Mode}
\label{sec:method:prompt}

A corrective signal admits four output types along two axes: \emph{output
kind} (natural language vs.\ numerical) and \emph{goal-conditioning}
(prompt mentions \texttt{desired\_goal} or not). The four are
\textsc{narrative}, \textsc{action}, \textsc{achieved\_goal}, and a
unified \textsc{all} JSON. The two position-emitting variants
(\textsc{achieved\_goal}, \textsc{all}) hand the VLM
\texttt{desired\_goal} as a literal numerical triplet; the cheapest
continuation when prompted for a corrective position in the same frame
is to copy it. No keyframe reasoning is needed.
We call this \textbf{teleport-collapse}: for a failed episode with
$\|\text{ag}_T-g\|_2\gg d_{\text{th}}\!=\!0.05$\,m, the VLM emits a
$\hat{p}$ that lies within $d_{\text{th}}$ of $g$ — a
trajectory-independent Dirac
$q_\phi(\hat{p}\!\mid\!\tau)=\delta(\hat{p}-g)$. The output carries
zero physical information and breaks HER's invariant.
The failure is a property of the schema's output type, not of any one
model. Table~\ref{tab:prompt-variants}: Opus~4.7 copies on $4/4$ of
FetchPush calls; GPT-4o copies on $4/6$ \textsc{all} calls but $0/6$ of
\textsc{achieved\_goal} calls. The verified-CF mechanism uses
\textsc{action}, where teleport is unrepresentable.

\subsection{VLM-Verified Counterfactual Hindsight}
\label{sec:method:verified}

To close the teleport loophole we ask the VLM for a 4-vector action
sequence and verify it inside a simulator fork. \textbf{Per-episode
protocol:}
(1) snapshot the MuJoCo state at the localized failure timestep $K$;
(2) fork a separate env, write the snapshot, call
\texttt{mujoco.mj\_forward};
(3) roll out the VLM-proposed action sequence for $N\!=\!50$ steps under
the \emph{unmodified} sparse reward;
(4) if $r_t\!\ge\!0$ fires at any step, append the trajectory plus a
hindsight-relabeled copy with confidence~1.0;
(5) otherwise reject and fall back to HER.
Under MuJoCo dynamics the 4-D end-effector action space cannot move a
50\,g block by 50\,cm in a 50-step rollout, so the teleport mode cannot
reach the buffer through this channel.

A 4/4 smoke check pins down each design assertion. Teleport
counterfactuals get killed before the first rollout step. Sensible-but-off
actions land at \texttt{final\_distance} 0.32\,m and 0.16\,m and are
likewise rejected. One hand-crafted near-goal action is verified at
$\text{final\_distance}\!=\!0.030$\,m. Snapshot round-trip equality holds
to \texttt{0.00e+00} on all four Fetch envs. CPU cost per verification:
17.6\,ms ($0.4\%$ overhead).

\paragraph{Asymmetric failure modes.}
The two channels rot differently when the VLM is wrong. Semantic PER
reads $q_\phi$ as a re-weighting prior: a miscalibrated VLM blows up
sampling variance, and the blow-up is bounded
(App.~\ref{app:bias-bound}). The verifier reads $q_\phi$ as a candidate
proposal: a miscalibrated VLM just gets rejected more often. The
buffer never sees a biased write either way, because what we actually
record is the env's own sparse reward, recomputed inside the rolled-out
verifier fork — not anything the VLM said about reward.

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:exp}

\paragraph{Setup.}
Three envs: Gymnasium-Robotics \texttt{FetchPush-v4},
\texttt{FetchPickAndPlace-v4}, \texttt{FetchSlide-v4}
(Fig.~\ref{fig:envs}, Appendix~\ref{app:methods}). Observation is 25-dim
goal-conditioned; action is 4-dim continuous (end-effector $\Delta xyz$
with gripper); reward is the built-in sparse indicator
$r_t=\mathbb{1}[\|\text{ag}_t-g\|_2\!\le\!0.05\text{m}]-1\!\in\!\{-1,0\}$
on a 50-step horizon. The agent is SAC~\citep{haarnoja2018sac}, two
256-unit ReLU hidden layers, $\gamma\!=\!0.98$, lr~$3\!\times\!10^{-4}$,
batch~256, HER \texttt{future} relabeling at $k\!=\!4$. We crank PER
with $\alpha\!=\!0.6$ and an annealed $\beta\colon0.4\!\to\!1.0$.
Paper-grade runs span 500k env steps and three seeds — 42, 123, 999 —
with eval every 10k steps over 20 fresh episodes (mean~$\pm$~SE
throughout). The VLM calls — Claude Opus~4.7, GPT-4o
(\texttt{gpt-4o-2024-08-06}), Sonnet~4.5 — go through the official APIs
at $\le\!\$0.05$/call and fire only on episodes the agent failed.

\paragraph{Horizon caveat.}
Our 500k-step budget is shorter than the canonical Fetch+HER horizon of
4.75M~\citep{plappert2018multigoal}. The comparisons we draw are between
\emph{methods at matched horizon}; cross-horizon bars in
Fig.~\ref{fig:headline} are sample-efficiency claims (more learning per
training step), not asymptotic dominance.

\subsection{Headline: Verified-CF matches VLM-CF, wins on Slide}
\label{sec:exp:headline}

\textbf{FetchPush (500k).} \emph{vlm\_cf} reaches $0.95\!\pm\!0.03$ and
\emph{verified\_cf} reaches $0.85\!\pm\!0.10$. Both clear HER@250k
($0.617$) by a wide margin and match the PER@3M asymptote
($\sim\!0.95$, from our 36-run prior ablation suite; see Contributions).
\textbf{FetchPickAndPlace (500k).} \emph{vlm\_cf} reaches
$0.367\!\pm\!0.08$ and \emph{verified\_cf} reaches $0.35\!\pm\!0.076$ —
tied within noise, both above HER@250k ($0.183$).
\textbf{FetchSlide (500k).} \emph{verified\_cf} posts the higher mean:
$0.617\!\pm\!0.017$ vs.\ \emph{vlm\_cf}'s $0.550\!\pm\!0.150$. The
$+0.067$ gap fits inside the wider SE bar at $n\!=\!3$ and we report
the two as approximately tied. Both move sharply above the prior
baselines: \emph{vlm\_cf} adds $+0.45$ over PER@3M ($0.10$) and
\emph{verified\_cf} adds $+0.434$ over HER@250k ($0.183$).

\textbf{Aggregate.} Across the three envs verified-CF averages $0.606$
and vlm\_cf averages $0.622$ ($\Delta\!=\!-0.016$). The two are tied
within seed noise. Verified-CF beats vlm\_cf on Slide and is slightly
behind on Push (where both methods sit near the horizon's ceiling). The
3M-step PER asymptote on Push ($\sim\!0.95$) comes from a prior 36-run
ablation suite (see \S\ref{sec:contributions}). The Slide gap is
consistent with HER's achieved-future relabel carrying little signal on
free-flight pucks. Gripper-to-object distance is non-monotone on Slide,
so a physics-consistent CF strike can supply a positive TD target that
HER's achieved-future relabel does not.

\subsection{Prompt design (head-to-head)}
\label{sec:exp:prompt}

A head-to-head Opus~4.7 vs.\ GPT-4o sweep on six bit-identical synthetic
Fetch failures (Table~\ref{tab:prompt-variants}) pins down the best
non-degenerate configuration. \texttt{(GPT-4o, achieved\_goal)}
teleport-collapses on $0/6$, against Opus's $4/4$ for the same
configuration. It takes the highest plausibility score on the
position-emitting axis ($0.79$) and is second on goal-progress ($0.83$,
behind only Opus's $0.97$ — but Opus's high score is attained by
literal goal-copying, a degenerate ``win''). GPT-4o still
teleport-collapses on $4/6$ \textsc{all} episodes, so the failure is
\emph{prompt-architectural}, not model-specific. The pivotal $0/6$ vs.\
$4/4$ contrast is judge-independent (decided by Euclidean predicate);
Fisher's exact test rejects equal proportions at $p\!<\!0.005$.
\S\ref{sec:exp:real} extends the grid to Sonnet~4.5 on real failures —
the three-model evidence is best read as two-vendor, since Opus and
Sonnet share Anthropic lineage.

\subsection{Cross-task transfer and real-data validation}
\label{sec:exp:real}

A 12-episode cross-task pilot ($4$ per env) with a single format-string
prompt template (differing only in one task-description sentence)
achieves 12/12 parse / task-relevance / plausibility on
Push/PickAndPlace/Slide; tolerant keyframe agreement is $9/12$
(Slide $4/4$, Push $3/4$, PickAndPlace $2/4$). A second sweep on
$n\!=\!10$ \emph{real} SAC-failure eval episodes confirms the
prompt-architectural fix transfers to a third VLM family
(Sonnet~4.5 teleports on $0/6$ PickAndPlace episodes under
\textsc{achieved\_goal}, vs.\ Opus~4.7's $4/4$ on the same variant) and
that the 5\,cm
reject-teleport gate fires on $12/20$ position-emitting generations
across both vendors (Appendix~\ref{app:results}).

\subsection{Pre-registered Oracle-CF kill experiment}
\label{sec:exp:kill}

18 SAC+HER and 18 Oracle-CF runs (3 envs $\times$ 3 seeds $\times$ 250k
steps) were executed
overnight.\footnote{Per-seed numbers from the
\texttt{path\_c\_overnight} W\&B sweep (commit \texttt{0fa36fc};
run-IDs listed at the GitHub repo's \texttt{REPRODUCE.md}).}
The pre-registered decision rule was: if
Oracle-CF exceeds HER by $\ge\!+0.10$ on FetchPickAndPlace, Path~C
proceeds; otherwise we pivot. Final eval success: on PickAndPlace, HER
$0.183\!\pm\!0.117$ vs.\ Oracle-CF $0.117\!\pm\!0.044$ ($\Delta\!=\!-0.066$,
\emph{below threshold}). On Push (post bug-fix, commit \texttt{ccb63d4}),
HER $0.617$ vs.\ Oracle-CF $0.383\!\pm\!0.109$. On Slide, HER $0.183$ vs.\
Oracle-CF $0.183$ ($\Delta\!=\!0.00$, below threshold).
\textbf{Path~C is killed at the 250k horizon}; the headline pivoted to
the IS-posterior framing (\S\ref{sec:method}) and the verifier
(\S\ref{sec:method:verified}). The 250k kill verdict is honored. A
post-hoc 1M re-run on PickAndPlace (Oracle-CF $0.583$ and HER $0.583$,
$n\!=\!3$; HER seed successes $0.35/0.45/0.95$) is reported as
transparency only: zero headroom over vanilla HER at convergence, and
\emph{vlm\_cf}@500k ($0.367$) reaches 63\% of HER@1M at half the
budget. It does not retract the kill.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Cold-start verifier-rejection regime (the main limitation).}
The simulator-as-verifier gate trades recall for precision by design.
What gets through is every time sparse-reward-positive. The price:
admission needs two events to coincide — VLM proposes a corrective
action, \emph{and} that action sequence crosses the 5\,cm threshold in
$\le\!N\!=\!50$ verifier steps. Early on, the joint event is rare. The
snapshot $\sigma$ sits far from $g$ because the policy hasn't learned
to bring the gripper near the object yet. FetchPickAndPlace seed~42
ran $100\%$ verifier-rejection over its first $\sim\!80$ VLM calls —
from the gradient's point of view that stretch is plain SAC+HER plus
VLM-API bills. The regime ends once the policy starts carrying the
gripper near the object: snapshots fall closer to $g$, the per-state
pass rate $m_{\text{ver}}(\sigma)$ climbs, the verifier admits
relabels, and the small handful that gets through delivers a
low-variance, physics-consistent training signal.
So the two methods rot asymmetrically. Semantic PER reads $q_\phi$ as a
re-weighting prior, and $q_\phi$-degeneracy turns into bounded variance
amplification. Verified-CF reads $q_\phi$ as a candidate proposal, and
$q_\phi$-degeneracy turns into signal extinction — unbounded in the
cold-start limit. Three knobs against this are pre-registered but not
run here: base-policy warm-up, longer $N$, and a soft acceptance
$r\!\ge\!-\rho_r$.

\paragraph{Oracle-CF kill bounds the headroom.}
The pre-registered kill (\S\ref{sec:exp:kill}) caps the headroom for any
VLM-in-replay method on Fetch at 250k steps. If perfect failure-frame
information plus a ground-truth corrective action cannot beat HER by
$+0.10$, then no realistic VLM (a $q_\phi$ with finite KL to that
oracle) will either. The bound is task-family-specific. Fetch is a
``HER-friendly'' family because the achieved-future relabel is a near-free
signal whenever the gripper-to-object distance is monotone, which it
typically is on Push and PickAndPlace. We pre-register an extension to
harder families (AntMaze, Adroit) where HER underperforms.

\paragraph{Other limitations.}
(i)~Our IS-correction is conservative (under-corrected $w_{\text{IS},P}$
rather than $w_{\text{IS},\text{Sem}}$); the bias is bounded
analytically, the fully-corrected ablation is pre-registered.
(ii)~Small-$n$ caveats: $n\!=\!6$ synthetic, $n\!=\!10$ real, $n\!=\!12$
cross-task; the 12/12 cross-task is not statistically distinguishable
from an 80\% true rate. We pre-register $n\!\ge\!30$ replications.
(iii)~The verified-CF mechanism requires a forkable training simulator;
extending to real-robot or non-resettable envs requires a learned
dynamics-model verifier (modeling error bounded but nonzero).
A faithful VLM-RB \citep{sharony2026vlmrb} reproduction is pre-staged in
our codebase for camera-ready head-to-head evaluation.

\paragraph{Conclusion.}
The multiplicative form of Semantic PER admits a clean IS correction.
The additive VLM-RB mixture does not — it silently retargets the Bellman
loss. VLM-Verified Counterfactual Hindsight asks the VLM for a
4-vector action sequence and accepts only sparse-reward-positive
rollouts, so the 100\%-teleport-collapse mode (Opus~4.7, FetchPush)
cannot reach the buffer at all. At 500k steps verified-CF averages
$0.606$ on the three Fetch envs ($0.85 / 0.35 / 0.617$ on
Push / PickAndPlace / Slide), ties VLM-CF in aggregate
($\Delta\!=\!-0.016$), and posts a higher mean on Slide ($+0.067$,
inside seed variance at $n\!=\!3$). The pre-registered Oracle-CF kill
on PickAndPlace at 250k ($\Delta\!=\!-0.066$) caps the credit-assignment
headroom on Fetch at this horizon. We honored it.
