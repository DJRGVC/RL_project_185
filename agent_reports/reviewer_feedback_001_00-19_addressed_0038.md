# NeurIPS 2024 Reviewer Feedback - Reviewer #1

**OVERALL: REJECT** (borderline reject; resubmittable with significant rework)

---

# Summary

The paper makes two methodological claims for sparse-reward manipulation on Gymnasium-Robotics Fetch: (1) VLM-guided experience replay can be recast as importance-sampled posterior reweighting where a frozen VLM emits an approximate posterior q_phi(t* | tau) over which timestep "caused" an episode's outcome, with Semantic PER taking a multiplicative form (mu_P * w_sem) that the authors claim uniquely preserves PER's IS correction; and (2) "VLM-Verified Counterfactual Hindsight" (VCH) closes a "teleport-collapse" failure mode by asking the VLM for a corrective action sequence rather than a hindsight goal and replaying it through a forked simulator that admits only physics-consistent relabels. Empirical content: a 3-seed SAC+HER comparison on three Fetch envs (Figure 1), a 16-episode prompt-design sweep across GPT-4o/Opus 4.7/Sonnet 4.5 (Tables 2 and pertaining figures), a 12-episode cross-task pilot, and a 4/4 smoke test of the verifier mechanism. The authors openly disclose in Section 7 that the headline GPT-4o curve in Figure 1 was run with a buggy pipeline (no teleport gate, all-variant prompt) that admitted ~67% degenerate counterfactuals as "verified" relabels, and the corrected runs were still in-flight at submission time.

# Strengths

1. **Honest disclosure of pipeline bug.** Section 7 ("The GPT-4o pipeline-bug discovery") is unusually forthright: the authors note that the headline figure conflates the genuine semantic signal with two-thirds-degenerate noise. This level of transparency is rare and commendable.

2. **The teleport-collapse phenomenon is a real, well-formalized observation.** Figure 4 (Definition: Teleport-collapse) gives a precise predicate (||p_hat - g||_2 <= d_th), the mechanistic account in Section 4.2 ("Why output type matters") is plausible, and the experimental verification in Table 2 (Opus 4.7 hits 100% teleport on achieved_goal vs. GPT-4o's 0%) is internally coherent. This is a useful negative finding for the VLM-as-counterfactual-oracle literature.

3. **Generator-verifier framing of VCH is conceptually clean.** Asking for an action (executable by env.step) rather than a goal (requires IK) is the right design choice: it removes both the position-copy bias and the inverse-kinematics dependency in one move. Figure 5's protocol (snapshot -> fork -> rollout -> accept-iff-sparse-fires) is well-specified at the algorithmic level.

4. **Off-policy correction taxonomy table (Table 1) is useful.** Placing Semantic PER alongside Retrace, V-trace, PER, and VLM-RB under a single "proposal/correction/bias-source" schema is a contribution to readers learning the off-policy-correction literature.

5. **Cross-task pilot uses a single prompt template.** The 12/12 parse rate / 12/12 task-relevance result (Section 6.4) is striking, though severely under-powered (see W3).

# Weaknesses

**W1. The headline empirical claim is unfalsified, and the paper knows it.** Figure 1 is "the headline ablation" but Section 7 admits the GPT-4o curve uses a buggy pipeline. The paper itself says "we expect the GPT-4o trajectory to track the heuristic Oracle far more tightly than Figure 1 suggests" — that is, the paper is asking reviewers to take the central empirical claim on faith pending overnight runs. By NeurIPS norms this disqualifies the headline. The authors should either (a) wait for the corrected runs before submission, (b) demote Figure 1 from "headline" to a clearly-labeled "pre-fix run, retained for transparency" and re-promote the prompt-design analysis (Section 6.3) as the headline, or (c) explicitly mark the figure with a watermark and a sentence in the caption indicating it is a known-buggy run. The current "Note: the GPT-4o curve uses a pre-fix pipeline..." sentence in the caption is buried and the figure is referenced approvingly elsewhere ("GPT-4o captures this headroom on PickAndPlace and narrows it on Push"). This is overclaiming on a known-broken artifact.

**W2. Three seeds is too few for the inference being drawn, and "non-overlapping SE intervals" is not a statistical test.** Section 6.1 (Evaluation protocol) states n=3 seeds per (method, env) cell with SE computed via t-distribution at df=2, and "Statistical comparison statements in the text use non-overlapping SE intervals as the threshold." This is multiply problematic: (i) df=2 makes the t-interval extremely wide and unstable; one outlier seed dominates; (ii) non-overlapping SE intervals is *not* a hypothesis test — it is a weaker condition than non-overlapping 95% CIs and substantially weaker than a paired t-test (cf. Cumming & Finch 2005, the standard reference); (iii) the recent RL replicability literature (Agarwal et al. 2021, *Deep RL at the Edge of the Statistical Precipice*, NeurIPS Outstanding Paper) explicitly demands stratified bootstrap CIs and IQM/Optimality Gap aggregation — neither appears here. Three seeds on three Fetch envs, with the known-bug caveat on top, supports almost no inferential claim. Either run >=5 seeds (10 is the field standard) and report IQM with bootstrapped CIs, or explicitly downgrade Figure 1's claims to "preliminary single-experiment trends."

**W3. The cross-task "12/12" claim is statistically uninformative and the paper concedes this but still leans on it.** Section 6.4 says "12/12 (100%)" parse/task-relevance/plausibility, but Section 7 ("Small-n caveats") concedes "the cross-task 12/12 is suggestive of strong transfer but not statistically distinguishable from an 80% true rate at n=12." Yet the abstract, intro, and conclusion all use the 12/12 number as load-bearing evidence: "a 12-episode cross-task pilot achieves 12/12 annotations across Push, PickAndPlace, and Slide with a single template — evidence that a foundation-VLM credit-assignment oracle generalizes where a learned priority head would not." The pilot supports neither (a) the comparison to a "learned priority head" (which is not run) nor (b) the inference about generalization (n=4 per task with a 100% rate is consistent with any true rate >=60% at standard alpha levels). Either remove from abstract/intro/conclusion or run the pre-registered n>=30 sweep before claiming the conclusion.

**W4. The "uniqueness of multiplicative" claim is mathematically thin.** Section 3 ("Why multiplicative?") asserts: "Any proposal modification mu'(i) = f(mu_P(i), q_phi(i;tau)) that wants the same correction interpretation must satisfy mu'(i) ∝ mu_P(i) * g(q_phi(i;tau))". This is not a theorem — it is a definitional choice. (i) "Wants the same correction interpretation" is undefined formally. (ii) The "uniqueness" follows only if you restrict to factorized forms a priori; many non-multiplicative reweightings (e.g., mu'(i) ∝ mu_P(i)^a * q^b with re-derived IS) also recover the uniform objective in suitable limits. (iii) The criticism of VLM-RB's additive form — "the beta->1 limit is the lambda-weighted objective ... not E_U[loss]" — is correct but applies just as readily to *any* mixture proposal, and the authors do not actually prove the lambda-weighted objective is worse for value-function learning; it could be better, and Sharony et al. report 11–52% gains over baselines. A claim of "unique" deserves a Lemma+Proof block, not a paragraph of natural-language hand-waving. Reduce this to "We choose a multiplicative form because it admits a clean IS-correction analysis (Eq. 2); the additive form of VLM-RB does not." Drop the uniqueness language.

**W5. The IS-correction analysis does not match the implementation, and the paper admits this.** Section 3 derives the strictly-correct w_IS,Sem = (|D_B| * mu_Sem)^(-beta), then Section 3 ("Off-policy correction taxonomy") confesses "In our current implementation we use the PER IS-weight w_IS,P rather than the strictly-correct w_IS,Sem. This is a conservative under-correction ... mathematically analogous to V-trace's ratio truncation: a controlled bias for variance reduction." This handwave is unsupported: V-trace's truncation has a known bias-variance tradeoff with theoretical analysis (Espeholt 2018, Lemma 1 + Theorem 1); the paper provides no analogous analysis for w_IS,P vs. w_IS,Sem. Limitation (i) in Section 7 explicitly says "the strictly-correct ablation has not yet been run." So the central theoretical claim (Section 3) describes an algorithm the paper does not implement, and the implemented algorithm has no theoretical guarantee. Either run the w_IS,Sem ablation and report it, or drop the "IS-corrected interpretation" claim from the abstract.

**W6. The verifier mechanism is not actually evaluated as an RL method.** Section 5 ("VLM-Verified Counterfactual Hindsight") describes a beautiful mechanism, and Section 6.7 reports a "4/4 smoke test on three failed episodes from C1_counterfactual_outputs.json plus a hand-crafted positive control." This is *not* an experiment — it is a unit test. The mechanism's value, namely whether it accelerates SAC training when integrated into the buffer, is in "in-flight experiments" (Section 6.6) at submission time. The paper makes promissory claims about Path C ("if Oracle-CF exceeds HER by >=+0.10 on FetchPickAndPlace eval success ...") but does not deliver them. As written, the VCH contribution is an unintegrated mechanism — interesting but unvalidated for the application it claims (improving sample efficiency on Fetch). Either run the integration and report, or reframe VCH as a "proposed mechanism, pilot-validated for correctness, integration deferred" — and accept that this halves the empirical contribution of the paper.

**W7. Differentiation from Sharony et al. 2026 (VLM-RB) is overstated.** Table differentiation table (Table on p2) lists five axes of difference but compresses two real differences (failure-direction signal; sim-fork verifier) into a marketing pitch. (i) The "Granularity: 32-frame sub-trajectory clip vs. single timestep ± window" claim is not obviously stronger — VLM-RB's clip-level signal averages over more visual context and may be more robust to single-frame VLM errors; the paper provides no ablation comparing window widths W. (ii) "Cross-task: per-benchmark tuning of lambda, TD multiplier vs. one prompt template across 3 Fetch envs" is a misleading apples-to-oranges: VLM-RB tunes a scalar; the present paper tunes prompts, output schema, gate radius, and oracle heuristic phases (a,b,c). Both involve per-benchmark tuning of different kinds. (iii) Sharony et al. report results on DQN/IQN/SAC/TD3 across MiniGrid and OGBench; the present paper reports SAC on three Fetch envs and never runs the present method on VLM-RB's benchmarks (or vice versa). A genuine differentiation would run Sem-PER on MiniGrid and VLM-RB on FetchPickAndPlace. Without that, "the multiplicative form is unique" (W4) is the only substantive differentiation, and it is mathematically thin.

**W8. The cross-task "keyframe agreement" gradient is reverse-engineered.** Section 6.4 ("Interpreting the keyframe-agreement gradient") observes monotonic agreement Slide(4/4) > Push(3/4) > PickAndPlace(2/4), and *then* concludes that low agreement on PickAndPlace reflects "heuristic under-localization rather than VLM error." This is a post-hoc rescue: the heuristic Oracle is also the "supervised target" for evaluating VLM agreement (Section 4 paragraph "Heuristic-Oracle upper envelope"). You cannot use the heuristic as both the ground-truth gold standard *and* the candidate-under-evaluation when convenient. A clean ablation: have a human annotator (the authors are fine) label failure timesteps on a held-out set of 20–30 episodes per task, then compare VLM-vs-human and heuristic-vs-human separately. Until then, the keyframe-agreement claim cannot resolve which of (a) VLM, (b) heuristic, or (c) the joint task is the source of disagreement.

**W9. Method clarity / reproducibility gaps.** Several pieces a reimplementer needs are missing or under-specified: (i) The semantic kernel K_W (Eq. for w_sem) is a hard-window indicator with bounded boost w_max, but the appendix says nothing about why W=5 and w_max=10 are the defaults beyond "11-step window with 10x multiplier"; no sweep over (W, w_max) is presented. (ii) The "sibling float64 array" for exact re-blending (Section 4.1 "Exact re-blending") is described in prose but no pseudocode is given. The closest is Algorithm 1 (Verified-CF, Appendix figure on page 18), which covers the verifier but not the Sem-PER replay update. (iii) Episode-end pipeline: when the VLM is called for *every* failed episode, what is the latency? The paper says CPU per-verification cost is 17.6 ms and 1700 failed episodes per 100k run, but the VLM call itself (network round-trip to GPT-4o or Anthropic) is presumably ~1–5 s and not amortized — that makes a 500k-step run blocked on ~50k * (1-3s) = 14–42 hours of VLM latency on top of training. This is not addressed. (iv) The bidirectional variant uses "the centre of the window over which ||ag_t - g|| dropped the most" for t_succ without defining the window length.

**W10. Figure quality issues.** (i) Figure 1: x-axis is "environment steps" but the unit (1k? 10k?) is not labeled in the caption. (ii) Figure 1 caption claims "GPT-4o captures this headroom on PickAndPlace and narrows it on Push" but the figure shows 3 seeds with SE — Section 6.1 admits this is non-overlapping-SE-interval logic, not a test. The Slide ranking "is within SE and not conclusive" is the only honest reading and should be applied symmetrically: are Push and PickAndPlace gaps actually outside SE for *all* time windows, or only at the last evaluation? (iii) Figure 6 (Cross-task transfer) shows three bar plots all at 100% — bars are uninformative when the metric is saturated; show the underlying per-episode scores or per-task variance estimates. (iv) Error bars are defined as "mean ± 1 SE" rather than 95% CI or the more modern bootstrap percentile interval (Agarwal 2021); use one of the latter. (v) Figure 1 lacks a legend for the dashed/solid line conventions across the three sub-plots. (vi) Tables 2 and 3 mix integer-counts (4/4, 6/6) with percentages and means±SE in the same column header line; cleaner separation would help.

**W11. The "in-flight experiments" decision rule (Section 6.6) is pre-registered but not yet adjudicated.** "If Oracle-CF exceeds HER by >=+0.10 on FetchPickAndPlace eval success, Path C ... proceeds to full VLM-CF sweeps; if Oracle-CF <= HER, we pivot the paper's headline to the IS-posterior framing." This is a published pre-registration without a result. At the camera-ready stage, this section should be one of (a) "Oracle-CF exceeded HER by X; we proceeded with VLM-CF and report Z" or (b) "Oracle-CF underperformed HER; we have pivoted to IS-posterior framing as the central contribution." The current state — promissory text in lieu of an adjudicated result — is unacceptable in a final submission.

**W12. Marketing-tone wording in places.** "Strictly dominates," "strictly more informative question," "unique proposal modification," "by construction," "the failure mode is structurally inexpressible" are deployed throughout (abstract, Sections 1, 3, 5). Several of these are technically defensible *only* with stronger formal arguments than the paper provides (see W4). Examples: (i) abstract "(GPT-4o, achieved_goal + 5 cm gate) as strictly dominant (0% collapse, 0.79 plausibility, 0.83 goal-progress)" — strict domination would require beating every alternative on every metric; Opus 4.7 ties on plausibility at 0.79 in the narrative variant and exceeds on goal-progress (0.97 vs 0.83) — the paper handles this by calling Opus's 0.97 "degenerate goal-copying," which is the *reason* for choosing GPT-4o but does not make GPT-4o strictly dominant on the original metric. (ii) Section 1: "the failure mode is structurally inexpressible" — true for the verbatim teleport mode at the action level, but the more general "VLM proposes a physically-reasonable but goal-irrelevant action" remains expressible and is in fact what Table 2 narrative/action rows show (0.42–0.53 goal-progress). Tone down throughout.

# Questions for Rebuttal

**Q1.** What are the corrected post-bug-fix Figure 1 numbers? The decision rule of Section 6.6 is pre-registered; please report the Oracle-CF vs HER delta on FetchPickAndPlace and state which branch of Path C was taken.

**Q2.** Why is w_IS,P used instead of w_IS,Sem (Section 3, Limitation i)? Specifically: please provide either (a) an empirical comparison of the two IS-weight choices on a single Fetch task or (b) a bias-variance analysis analogous to Espeholt et al. 2018 Lemma 1 / Theorem 1 for the V-trace truncation. Without one of these, the "conservative under-correction" claim is unmotivated.

**Q3.** What is the wall-clock VLM-call latency per failed episode, and how is it amortized during the 500k-step run? If VLM calls are ~1–3 s each and 10k calls are needed, the run is blocked on hours of API latency unless calls are batched or off-trajectory. Please describe the actual pipeline.

**Q4.** Have you compared (W=5, w_max=10) to the natural alternatives (W=2, 10, 25; w_max=2, 5, 25)? A single (W, w_max) sweep on FetchPush with the corrected pipeline would dramatically strengthen the method-design contribution.

**Q5.** What is the human-vs-VLM agreement on failure-timestep localization (W8)? If author annotation is available, please report VLM-vs-human and heuristic-vs-human separately on at least 20 episodes per task.

**Q6.** Why not run the present method on at least one of VLM-RB's benchmarks (MiniGrid or OGBench), or VLM-RB on at least one Fetch env? Without this, the differentiation claims (Table differentiation) are not directly testable.

# Limitations to Add or Strengthen

**L1.** The pre-registered headline result is not in. Section 6.6 should either (a) report the post-bug-fix numbers or (b) be promoted to a Limitation explicitly: "This submission is at the pre-result stage of the pre-registration; the main empirical claim is conditional on the in-flight runs."

**L2.** Section 7 Limitation (v) notes "does not yet cover open-weights VLMs" but does not note the more pressing limitation: a 3-VLM "family robustness" claim from one closed-source vendor (Anthropic Opus, Anthropic Sonnet) and one (OpenAI GPT-4o) is, in family terms, n=2 — Opus and Sonnet share much of the same pre-training corpus and RLHF lineage. Reframe as "two-family" not "three-family."

**L3.** The VCH mechanism's cost model is incomplete: 17.6 ms CPU per verification is reported but the VLM action-proposal latency, the sim-fork instantiation cost (MuJoCo state restore is non-trivial), and the failure rate of the verifier (how often does a proposed action sequence get rejected) are not reported. Add a Cost-Accounting subsection.

# Scores (NeurIPS 2024)

- **Soundness:** 2 (fair) — the IS-posterior framing is interesting but the implementation under-corrects, the central empirical figure runs on a buggy pipeline, three-seed evaluations with non-overlapping SE intervals are insufficient inferential basis, and uniqueness claims (W4) lack rigor.

- **Presentation:** 3 (good) — the paper is well-structured, Section 7 is honest about the bug, and Tables 1–3 are useful. Marketing tone (W12) and figure quality (W10) drag.

- **Contribution:** 2 (fair) — the teleport-collapse phenomenon and the generator-verifier framing are genuinely useful conceptual contributions; the IS-posterior recasting is a reframing more than a novel theoretical result; the verified-CF mechanism is well-designed but unintegrated as an RL method.

- **Overall:** 4 (borderline reject) — the contribution would be 6 if the headline Figure 1 ran on the corrected pipeline and Section 6.6's pre-registration were adjudicated. As is, the paper has the bones of a NeurIPS-quality submission but is shipped before its central empirical claim has been validated.

- **Confidence:** 4 (confident; I am familiar with PER, HER, off-policy correction, and the recent VLM-as-reward / VLM-as-replay literature).

# Top-3 Fixes for Next Iteration

1. **Run and report the corrected Figure 1 ablation, with n>=5 seeds and bootstrap CIs (IQM aggregation per Agarwal 2021).** This is non-negotiable: the central empirical claim cannot rest on a known-buggy pipeline, and "non-overlapping SE intervals at df=2" is not a defensible inferential bar. Decision: either ship corrected numbers, or demote Figure 1 from "headline" and re-promote the prompt-design analysis (Section 6.3) as the empirical center of the paper.

2. **Either prove the multiplicative-form uniqueness as a Lemma+Proof, or downgrade the claim.** Section 3's natural-language "any proposal modification that wants the same correction interpretation must satisfy mu'(i) ∝ mu_P(i) * g(q_phi(i;tau))" needs (a) a formal definition of "same correction interpretation," (b) a precise statement of the function class over which uniqueness holds, and (c) a proof. Also: run the strictly-correct w_IS,Sem ablation on at least one Fetch task to validate the under-correction analogy to V-trace.

3. **Validate VCH as an RL method, not a unit test.** A 4/4 smoke test is necessary but not sufficient. Integrate VCH into the SAC+HER pipeline and report eval-success curves (n>=5 seeds, 250k+ steps) for SAC+HER vs SAC+HER+VCH on at least FetchPickAndPlace. If integration is not feasible in time, reframe Section 5 explicitly as "mechanism design; integration deferred to future work" and remove "VLM-Verified Counterfactual Hindsight" from the title to reflect that the title's second contribution is at the pilot stage.
